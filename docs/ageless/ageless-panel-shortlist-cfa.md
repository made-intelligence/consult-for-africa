## Three findings that shaped the casting

**There is no Nigerian longevity medicine profession to recruit from.** No society, no verifiable Nigerian physician with A4M or ABAARM certification, and no Lagos clinic practising longevity medicine as medicine rather than as drip retail. What does exist, organised and board certified, is lifestyle medicine, through the Society of Lifestyle Medicine of Nigeria. Fill the seat from there and it is credible.

**Menopause medicine in Nigeria is genuinely thin, and it is documented.** A published survey found Nigerian gynaecologists largely manage severe menopausal symptoms with reassurance and anxiolytics rather than hormone therapy, and SOGON has no menopause interest group. The subject this room most wants covered has almost nobody holding it.

**Do not cast for reach.** Outside one name at 2.4 million there is no Nigerian clinical wellness voice at influencer scale, and confirmed counts here run to four figures. Seventy guests come from your own list and from Capital Club, not from a panellist's following.

## Added at your request, with what we found

**Joycee Awosika**, founder and chief executive of the ORÍKÌ Group. Note the spelling. She runs a wellness business of real scale: ten years old, somewhere between thirteen and fifteen spas plus six hair restoration centres across several African countries, with her own manufacturing in Nigeria, a training institute and an app. Roughly thirty three thousand on Instagram, and genuine international standing through the World Economic Forum and the Presidential Precinct.

She is a strong name and we would want her in the room. We would not put her on this panel, for two reasons.

The first is register. She is an energy economist by training, with a career at Constellation Energy, DFID and the Ministry of Power before ORÍKÌ. She has no health credential, and on a stage with three consultant physicians and a board certified plastic surgeon she would be the only person with no clinical training. Her public themes are natural ingredients, building an African wellness industry, vocational training and entrepreneurial structure. We found nothing from her on hormones, metabolic health, menopause or the biology of ageing.

The second is commercial. ORÍKÌ is a spa chain and a skincare line in Lagos. That is directly adjacent to a longevity and aesthetics centre, and she would be selling a category the host also sells.

**Our suggestion.** Invite her as a guest, and ask her to co-host or convene rather than to sit on the panel. A wellness industry founder of her standing bringing her own list is worth more to this evening than a fifth chair, and it avoids both problems at once.

## Before anybody is approached

Three names are CFA relationships and need a decision from Debo rather than a telephone call: **Dr Chito Nwana** at Arabella, **Dr Uju Rapu** at Belfiore, and **Iwosan** through Dr Oluwatomi Kogo, which is also adjacent competition. **Grandville** appears nowhere here, because it is simultaneously the direct competitor and a live CFA marketing prospect.

Six entries carry an unverified flag, almost all on whether somebody is still in the post stated. Verify before a name goes on anything printed, because a panellist billed with the wrong hospital is the kind of error a room of clinicians notices.

**Two risks sit on the stage rather than on the list.** HEFAMAA has scope of practice live in Lagos right now, so a panel drifting into who may inject is a regulatory moment, not merely a lively one. And a clinician praising a named treatment from a stage you host is closer to an advertisement than it feels, with the ARCON liability sitting on the advertiser. Brief the chair on both.

**One bench you already have.** MedLYFE's own regenerative medicine conference in February 2026 fielded Dr Adedotun Ajelabi, Dr Itunu Akinware and the Lagos Commissioner for Health. If a seat stays empty, that bench is warm.
