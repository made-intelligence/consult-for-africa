## The rule that governs who we ask

Nobody is paid to bring us a patient, and nobody on this panel is there to sell. A moderator's honorarium is a different thing and is perfectly proper.

The two highest-value seats are the ones that look least commercial: a teaching-hospital clinician and a chair the room already respects. A panel of practice owners reads as a trade show.

## Three things the research changed

**There is no Nigerian longevity medicine profession to recruit from.** No Nigerian longevity society, no verifiable Nigerian physician with A4M or ABAARM certification, and no Lagos clinic practising longevity medicine as medicine rather than as drip retail. What does exist, and is organised and board certified, is **lifestyle medicine**: the Society of Lifestyle Medicine of Nigeria, founded 2017, recognised by the NMA, members certified by the International Board of Lifestyle Medicine. Fill the longevity seat from there and the seat is credible. Call it longevity and it is not.

**Menopause medicine in Nigeria is genuinely thin, and that is documented rather than a gap in the searching.** A published survey found Nigerian gynaecologists largely manage severe menopausal symptoms with reassurance and anxiolytics rather than hormone therapy. SOGON has no menopause interest group. There is no Lagos woman gynaecologist with a public menopause practice. The subject your room most wants covered has almost nobody holding it, which is an opportunity as much as a problem.

**Do not cast for reach.** Outside Aproko Doctor at 2.4 million, there is no Nigerian clinical wellness voice at influencer scale. Confirmed counts on this list run to four figures. The room comes from your own list and from Capital Club, not from a panellist's following.

## Two risks that sit on the stage rather than on the list

**Scope of practice is a live regulatory question in Lagos right now.** HEFAMAA has already convened dermatologists, dentists and aesthetics practitioners in a formal meeting with the dermatology, plastic surgery and aesthetic medicine associations present. If the panel drifts into who is allowed to inject, that is a regulatory moment on your stage rather than merely a lively one. Brief the chair to steer round it.

**What a panellist says about MedLYFE's services from the stage carries advertising risk.** ARCON polices health advertising and the liability sits with the advertiser. A clinician praising a named treatment from a stage you are hosting is closer to an advertisement than it feels in the room. Brief the panel to discuss categories, not the host's menu.
