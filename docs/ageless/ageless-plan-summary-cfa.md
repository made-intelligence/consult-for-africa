## The one job

Put seventy of the right people in a room at Capital Club on 21 October, and convert what happens there into a diary afterwards.

Everything else is subordinate to that. The evening is not the outcome: a full room that produces nothing on the 22nd is a party, and a half-empty one that produces fifteen consultations is a success.

Cutting from 130 to 70 helps more than it costs. Seventy is a room where people talk to each other and the clinical team can reach everybody, and it means every seat has to be worth something.

---

## What the evening has to do

AGELESS is the platform and it is built to recur. The proposition is a new era of health, beauty and longevity. The panel is *The New Science of Ageing Well*. The fireside is *The Art of Looking Like Yourself*, with Dr Chinwe Kpaduwa. Keeping those four names apart is what lets edition two reuse everything but the panel.

Medlyfe hosts, in the footer. That is a regulatory position rather than a courtesy: Medlyfe is the licensed, bookable entity. No practice is introduced, because the practice name that was being used does not exist. Dr Kpaduwa is named as a clinician and sets the clinical standard. Every piece of copy already survives that distinction and it should hold on the night.

## What is already built and live

The event page is live at consultforafrica.com/lyfe, carrying the RSVP and a fallback discovery call, both writing into one queue. The coordinator queue at /admin/lyfe-enquiries sorts by how long somebody has waited rather than when they arrived. Invitations are built in individual and group versions, personalised per guest, as print, feed and WhatsApp Status, including Dr Itunu's own with her name above the invitee. Alongside them sit six feed pieces, five Status pieces, a salon flyer, a hand-over card and a scope sheet for referring clinicians. The panel shortlist is a separate document, four candidates for each of six jobs, ready to mark up.

The page is deliberately kept out of search while the brand has no domain of its own. Links shared by hand still work.

## Where seventy guests come from

Nobody is coming off an advertisement. Seventy is a hand-built list, and the only question is who owns each tranche.

| Source | Roughly | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MedLYFE's patients and members | 25 | By telephone, from the consented book |
| Dr Itunu's and Dr Chinwe's own lists | 15 | Personally. A principal's forwarded invitation outperforms everything else here |
| A convening partner, most likely GAIA | 12 | Needs a decision this week. Their membership is this exact room |
| Referring clinicians | 10 | Guests, not a sales target. Next year's referral flow |
| Capital Club members | 5 | Ask the club. Their list overlaps ours |
| Press | 3 | Two interview slots in a side room |

The mechanic that actually fills a room is a printed invitation delivered by hand against a telephone call already made. An email does not do it. The invitations are built to be personalised one per guest for exactly that reason.

| Panel chair | Four candidates. First call Adesuwa Onyenokwe |
| Fireside host | Four candidates. First call Ezinne Akudo |

Three calls matter more than the rest: the chair, because it decides whether the evening is a conversation or four presentations; the longevity seat, because it is what makes this an evening about ageing rather than about treatments; and the skin seat, because an academic there keeps the aesthetic half clinical.

Take the skin seat from the teaching hospitals, not from private clinics. Every private aesthetic dermatologist in Lagos sells what MedLYFE sells, and a rival with a microphone is worse than an empty chair.
