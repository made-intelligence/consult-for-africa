## The evening

AGELESS is a cocktail evening at the Capital Club, Lagos, for seventy guests. Mostly Lagos women and men over forty who already spend on how they look and feel, and who have never had the whole subject put in front of them by people who actually know. Medlyfe hosts. It introduces Dr Chinwe Kpaduwa and Lyfe Plastics and Dermatology.

It is a conversation, not a sales floor, and the room will tell the difference inside five minutes.

The argument running through the night is that how you feel and how you look are one appointment and not two. The panel takes the inner. The fireside turns outward.

## The opening address

**Dr Adedotun Ajelabi, Clinical Lead, Medlyfe. Fifteen minutes.**

You set the frame for everything after you. The room should leave your fifteen minutes understanding that ageing is now something medicine has a range of answers to, running from the everyday to the surgical, and that the rest of the evening walks along that range.

What you will be asked to cover:

- What can medicine actually do about how we age now that it could not do ten years ago?
- Where does the everyday end and the medical begin? Sleep and food at one end, an operation at the other, and what sits between.
- What is genuinely established, and what is being oversold to this audience right now?
- What does Medlyfe do with that range, in practice, for somebody who walks in on a Tuesday?

## The panel

**The New Science of Ageing Well.** What changes in your body, brain and skin after 40, and what you can actually do about it.

Forty five minutes, chaired by Dr Itunu Akinware. About nine minutes of speaking each. Experiential rather than clinical: the room wants to know what to do on Monday morning, delivered with the authority of someone who actually knows.

**One rule, and it is the only one that matters.** Every answer ends in something a guest can act on. Not a principle, not a caution, something they could begin this week. The chair will come back to anyone who finishes on a generality.

### Dr Timi Adenuga, on metabolism, weight and the new drugs

- Why does the same diet stop working at forty five when it worked at thirty?
- The drugs everybody in this room has already googled. Who are they actually for, who should not touch them, and what does the honest version of the result look like?
- What happens when somebody stops taking them?
- Is surgery ever the right first answer rather than the last one?
- If someone leaves tonight wanting to act, what is the first thing they should do?

### Gbemi Giwa, on food and body composition

- Can you eat the food we actually eat and still change your body? What does that look like on a real Lagos plate?
- What do people get wrong in the first month, every time?
- A woman of forty five who has never lifted anything. Where does she start, and what changes first?
- What does a realistic twelve weeks look like, against what the internet promises?
- What costs nothing and works?

### Joycee Awosika, on vitality and energy

- Ten years of building a wellness business in this market. What do Nigerian women actually buy, and what do they say they want?
- Where is the gap between those two, and what does it tell us?
- What is being sold in Lagos right now that does not work?
- What is the most underrated thing people could do for their energy, that nobody will sell them because there is no money in it?

### Dr Folake Kofo-Idowu, on menopause and hormones

- What actually changes, and when does it really start? Most of this room thinks it is a thing that happens at fifty.
- What should a woman of forty be measuring, and how often?
- Hormone therapy. What is the honest state of the evidence, and why is it so hard to get properly in Nigeria?
- What do Nigerian doctors most often get wrong about perimenopause?
- A woman here tonight recognises herself in what you have said. What does she do tomorrow?

## The fireside

**The Art of Looking Like Yourself. Dr Chinwe Kpaduwa with Dr Debo Odulana. Twenty five minutes.**

Not a presentation and not an interview about procedures. It is about judgement, which is the thing the room cannot buy anywhere else in Lagos that evening.

- Your position, over two years of writing, is that the point is to look like yourself. What does that mean in practice, and what does it rule out?

## What the chair is doing

Dr Akinware is not introducing four talks. She is keeping one argument moving across four people, cutting in when an answer drifts into generality, and making sure every panellist is asked something only they can answer.

She will also move the conversation on if it drifts into who should be allowed to perform what, which is a live regulatory question in Lagos and not an evening's entertainment.

## What nobody on this stage may say

Five regulators have an interest in an evening like this and one of them has real teeth.

**ARCON** requires advertising to be approved before it is seen, carries a minimum fine of five hundred thousand naira, and places the liability on the advertiser rather than the speaker. Anything said from this stage that reads as an advertisement lands on Medlyfe. So: **no named prescription products.** Not toxins, not fillers, not weight loss drugs by brand. Describe what a class of treatment does if you must, and let people ask privately afterwards.

**MDCN** treats self advertisement by a doctor as a disciplinary matter. The clinicians here are educating; the clinic is the thing that advertises. In practice, do not pitch your own practice from the stage. You will be introduced properly and the room will find you at the bar.

**No before and after claims, and no outcome promises.** "Most people see" is a claim. "In my experience, some people find" is an observation. The difference is the whole of it.

**Scope of practice is live with HEFAMAA right now.** A panel that drifts into who may inject becomes a regulatory moment rather than a lively one.

None of this makes for a duller evening. In front of a Lagos audience that has heard every claim already, the most trusted thing you can do is be the person who will not make one.
