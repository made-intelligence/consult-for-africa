## What the film has to do

A guest arrives at a cocktail evening knowing MedLYFE is a name on an invitation. Ninety seconds later they should understand that it is a medical centre, that it measures things, and that what it does is available to them.

It opens the evening and hands over to Dr Adedotun Ajelabi, who then speaks for ten minutes on whether we can live to 100 and still live well. The film sets that up. It does not answer it.

- **It is not a clinic tour.** Rooms are the setting, not the subject.
- **It is not a list of services.** Naming nine therapies in ninety seconds reads as a menu.
- **It is not aspirational stock.** No sunrises, no yoga on a beach, no stethoscope on a desk.

### What she says

Three lines, learned rather than read. No autocue if it can be avoided; an earpiece is better than a wandering eye.

> **One.** Most of us find out something is wrong after it has already happened. That is the part medicine can now change.

> **Two.** We measure what is actually going on in your body. Your metabolism, your hormones, your inflammation, your risk. Then we tell you what to do about it, and we are specific.

> **Three.** Not to live forever. To be well for as long as you live.

If she would rather say it in her own words, that is better than these words. The sense is what matters: we measure, we are specific, and the goal is healthspan rather than lifespan.

## What must not be in it

This is the part that carries a fine, so it is written out rather than assumed.

- **No patients.** Nobody who could be read as a patient appears in any frame. Staff only, and only as hands and backs of heads.
- **No before and after.** None filmed, none supplied, none used.
- **No drug or product brand names.** NAFDAC does not permit prescription medicines to be advertised to the public by name. Say what something does, never what it is called.
- **No claim of a result.** Nothing that promises an outcome, a timescale or a cure.
- **No comparison to another clinic**, named or implied.
- **Dr Ajelabi speaks for the centre, not for herself.** The MDCN treats a practitioner advertising themselves as a disciplinary matter. She is introduced by role, and the centre is the advertiser.

One further point, for the website version only. A film shown to an invited room on one evening is not the same thing as a film running on a public website. The website version is an advertisement and sits inside ARCON's remit, which means pre-exposure approval and a licensed agency to file it. Liability is on the advertiser, not on you. Flag it to us before the hero version goes live rather than after.
