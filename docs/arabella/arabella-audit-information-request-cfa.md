## A. The company and the change of name

Already in: the certificate of incorporation.

- The CAC status report for Arabella, with the current directors and shareholders, and whether any shareholder is not Nigerian
- Tabitha Medical Centre's registration: a company or a business name, its number, and the plan and timing for closing it
- What still sits in Tabitha's name and needs moving: bank accounts, HMO contracts, the facility registration, the premises lease, signage and stationery, the website and domain, the social media accounts and the phone lines. A short list is enough
- What has already been told to HMOs, staff, suppliers and patients about the change, and in what form
- Any tax, PAYE or pension position left open in Tabitha's name
- How decisions are made: who meets, how often, who signs off spending above what amount, and the bank mandate
- Any loan, overdraft, equipment finance, or money you have put in personally, with its terms
- Any other clinician with a share of the revenue or of the business

## B. Licences, insurance and compliance

- The facility registration with the FCT health authority: the category, the name it is in, the expiry date and any conditions, plus any inspection report
- The medical director of record for the premises
- MDCN practising licences for every doctor who works here, including visiting consultants and the anaesthetist, and Nursing and Midwifery Council licences for every nurse and midwife
- For the fertility service: the responsible clinician, the embryologist's qualification and registration, and any accreditation held
- Pharmacy premises licence and superintendent pharmacist; the controlled drugs register and the last reconciliation
- Medical Laboratory Science Council registration for laboratory work done on site
- Radiation licence and dosimetry, if any X-ray equipment is held
- Professional indemnity for every clinician and for the facility; public liability, fire and equipment insurance
- Clinical waste contract and consignment notes; fire safety certificate
- Registration with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission, and who the data protection officer is
- Any current or threatened claim, complaint to a regulator, or litigation

## C. Money in

Already in: the sales record, January to September 2026.

- The sales record for October to December 2025, so that we have a full twelve months
- The summary sheet for August and September 2026
- Bank statements, as in item 1 above, and whether any business money has been paid into a personal or staff account
- The two largest payments this year, March's N6.5m for fertility and September's N2m procedure: what each was for, and whether either was paid in instalments
- Registrations: the patient list shows 145 new registrations this year and the sales record 83 registration fees. Is the fee waived for some patients, and for whom?
- Cash handling: who takes money at the front desk, how it is receipted, when it is banked and who checks it against the day
- Any money owed to Arabella by patients, and how it is chased

## D. Prices, packages and who pays

Already in: the price list and the list of HMOs.

- Prices for the procedures marked TBD and for fertility treatment, including what a cycle includes, how drugs are charged and what a frozen transfer costs
- What each maternity package includes and, precisely, what it excludes: what happens on a caesarean, a longer stay, a baby needing admission, a transfer out, or twins
- How a package is paid, and what happens when a patient stops paying part way through
- Who may give a discount, and the discounts given this year
- For each HMO: the tariff, the contract and its date, the contact person, whether it is active or lapsed, and whether it is in Arabella's or Tabitha's name
- HMO and company receivables: what each owes and for how long, claims rejected this year with the reasons, and who chases them
- The company accounts in the patient list, including Central Bank of Nigeria, ECOWAS, Shoprite, Prowess Energy and the International Community School: the terms of each, and whether they are still active
- How an authorisation code is obtained, and what the front desk does when it is slow
- The last five quotes that did not turn into treatment, and what is known about why

## E. Money out

- Monthly costs for the last 12 months, as in item 2 above, in whatever form they are kept
- Payroll by person and month, including locums and visiting consultants, and any commission or bonus
- Purchases of drugs and consumables with supplier and price, and any supplier credit or money owed
- What laboratory tests sent out cost, by month
- The café: what it takes and what it costs to run
- Marketing spend in the last 12 months, by channel, including the social media and podcast teams
- Rent and the lease terms; diesel and power; equipment and its finance
- Any management accounts, profit and loss or balance sheet, and the accountant's details

## F. How each service runs

**Maternity.** Women booked and babies delivered this year, booked women who delivered elsewhere and why, caesarean rate, the route for transferring a mother or baby who needs intensive care and the last three times it was used, how quickly blood reaches theatre in a haemorrhage, and anaesthetic cover out of hours.

**Fertility.** Cycles started, cancelled before transfer, transfers and pregnancies this year by age band; who does the embryology; consent forms for storing eggs and embryos; how the storage tanks are monitored and what happens in a power cut.

**Gynaecology surgery.** Procedures done this year, cancellations on the day and why, and who provides anaesthesia and theatre staff.

**Laboratory.** Tests done on site and tests sent out, which laboratory receives them, turnaround time, and the price Arabella pays for each.

**Pharmacy.** The stock register with a unit cost for each item, purchases this year, stockouts in the last three months, expired stock written off, and how the cold chain is kept.

**Wellness.** What is offered, by whom, and how it is paid.

## G. Clinical safety

- Written protocols for postpartum haemorrhage, pre-eclampsia and eclampsia, shoulder dystocia, newborn resuscitation and sepsis, and when each was last practised
- The resuscitation trolley check, and life support training with expiry dates
- Consent forms for delivery, surgery and fertility treatment
- Incident reports this year and what changed as a result; any maternal or newborn death, stillbirth or unexpected transfer in the last two years, and the review that followed
- Sterilisation: autoclave validation and load records
- Infection prevention: the last audit, cleaning schedules and sign-off
- Complaints received in any form, including on social media, and how each was answered

## H. Patients and where they come from

Already in: the patient list.

- What patients have been told about how their details are used, and any consent or privacy notice they signed
- Whether anyone has asked not to be contacted, and how it is recorded
- How a follow-up appointment is booked before a patient leaves, and who checks that she came back
- The patient journey as it runs: who answers the phone and WhatsApp, how a booking is confirmed, waiting times, and who follows up
- Opening hours, including weekends
- Where new patients come from: which doctors, clinics, companies or people refer, how many each sent this year, and anyone who used to refer and has stopped
- Enquiries a month by channel, and how many become a booking
- HERcast: listeners by platform, and any sponsorship
- Any outreach already sent to the patient list, when, and what came back

## I. People

Already in: the staff list.

- Contracts and job descriptions, and the staff handbook or policies if any
- The rota for the last three months, and how nights, weekends and holidays are covered
- Who covers the front desk, and who covers when that person is away
- Staff who left in the last two years, from which role, and why
- Training records and any appraisal or performance conversations
- How tasks are given out and checked: who decides what needs doing, how the person doing it knows, and how anyone knows it was done

## J. Systems and records

- Every system in use, what it is for, who supplies it and what it costs, including the one the patient list came from
- Who administers each, who holds the passwords, and whether any login is shared
- What is backed up, where, and when a restore was last tested
- Who can see patient records, and whether access can be traced to a person
- Any record kept only on a personal phone, email account or drive
- The website: who built it, who holds the domain, and who can publish
- Google Business Profile, Google Ads and Meta Business accounts: who owns them and who has admin
