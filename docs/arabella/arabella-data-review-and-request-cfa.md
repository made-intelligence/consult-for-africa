## 1. What the additional information shows

| Interim report | With the additional information |
|---|---|
| Arabella took N48.7m, about N5.4m a month, January to September | N48.7m is what the sales record captures. Clinical receipts across the three bank accounts are about N58m to N62m. Our best estimate of gross clinical income is about N6.5m a month (range N5.4m to N6.9m), against about N4.8m a month in 2025 |
| July and August show the second half falling away | The sales record understates July. Across all three accounts July was N5.7m and September N10.8m. Only August (N3.4m) was weak, and July to September was above April to June |
| Without its large payments, a month runs N1.8m to N6.2m | The range is N1.8m to N7.5m. September without its largest payment was the second-best month |
| Maternity packages are 6% of income, two to four packages | Not supported. The "packages" column is mostly N45,000 to N50,000 items that are not maternity. Antenatal visits are recorded as consultations and the delivery as an admission, so maternity income cannot be separated in the sheet |
| 7 of 11 maternity bookings delivered elsewhere, so mothers are lost at delivery | Your note says these mothers travelled outside to deliver. That can mean a planned choice to deliver outside Abuja or abroad. The records do not show that they were lost to a competitor |
| 72% of patients paid once, so follow-up is not being booked | The sheet records payments, not visits. Instalments, prepaid packages, free follow-up within a week, insurer visits and name spellings all distort it. The figure does not support that conclusion |
| About 22 new patients a month; Friday to Sunday are quiet | Both reflect how names and dates were entered in the sheet rather than patient behaviour |
| Operating cost of about N40.0m ran above revenue of N37.4m, January to July | Not supported. The expense summary tabs are incomplete and include items that are not operating costs. On the full records the January to July result is close to break-even, and the records cannot fix whether it is slightly positive or slightly negative |
| Arabella expenses N10.2m, mostly from July | The summary tab leaves out February to June. The line items total N24.2m, most of it fit-out in April |
| Two salary rolls ran together from July | One workforce, on one staff list, was paid from two accounts throughout the year. There is no second payroll and no double counting |
| Neither account retains anything; no working balance on 2 October | 2 October is the low point after month-end payroll. The outflows include N36.75m placed with an asset manager in February and about N46m invested in the Asokoro premises and equipment. Cash was turned into an investment and a building, not consumed |
| The pack does not name a specialist obstetrician | Your staff list names four visiting consultant obstetricians and gynaecologists, a consultant anaesthetist and a nurse anaesthetist. What we do not hold is their licences and qualifications |
| The 2026 renewal was paid but has not been granted | You applied and paid before the deadline, the committee receipted and stamped the form, and your medical association endorsed it. We have not seen the certificate, which is all we can say |
| Three payer agreements received | One is signed by both parties (Reliance, 2020), one awaits countersignature (Allianz Partners, 2026) and one is a blank template |
| The laboratory is properly licensed | The one laboratory licence in the pack is for a scientist who is not on the staff list |
| The fertility line earns a 42% margin | It is one case in March. It cannot be read as a margin on a service line |
| Pause dispensing until a pharmacist is appointed | The right first step is to confirm the pharmacist arrangement |

## 2. What is unchanged

- Founder dependency: you, Tolu and a member of staff each named it independently.
- Staff survey: patient safety rated highly; speaking up to senior colleagues is the weakest area.
- Front desk: patient cases discussed within hearing of the waiting area.
- Start time: the working day often begins nearer 9am than 8am.
- Insurer income is small: N1.13m from January to September 2026, then N1.15m in the first 13 days of October.
- Patient list: 4,379 records, of which about 14% carry a phone number. About 3,068 carry a real email address (476 use a shared placeholder).
- Contact before marketing: patients registered as Tabitha should be told about the change of name and given an opt-out first.
- Registration: no document in the pack records Asokoro with the registration committee. Your Allianz Partners agreement quotes the existing registration number for Asokoro. Whether a move and change of name is an amendment to that registration or needs a new one is a question for the committee, and we should confirm it together.
- Pharmacy: no pharmacist appears in the pack, while the Allianz tariff prices pharmacist services.
- Income concentration: the largest ten payers are about 40% of receipts in the sales record.

## 4. Additional data request

Priority 1 settles the revenue and cost questions and is the most useful to send first. Exports straight from a system are better than retyped summaries.

### Income and patients

1. Priority 1: a billing export from the records system, January to October 2026, both sites, one row per item: patient number, date, service, payer, amount billed, amount paid, payment channel.
2. Priority 1: confirmation of which sites and accounts the sales record covers, and whether Asokoro collections are in it.
3. Priority 1: the Access Bank statement, January 2025 to date, and any other account, POS terminal or wallet that receives patient payments.
4. The cash book, or a note of how cash is recorded and banked.
5. First Bank merchant settlement reports for 2026, to reconcile POS income.
6. What the regular N395,500 monthly receipt from March to September is.

### Costs and funding

7. Priority 1: a complete expense ledger for both entities, January to October 2026, from the line items, with each line marked operating or capital.
8. Priority 1: payroll schedules, January to October 2026, names and amounts, from both accounts.
9. A register of the money you and others have put into the business in 2025 and 2026, with the terms of each: loan or capital, interest, repayment.
10. Source documents for the large receipts of 4 February (N30m), 9 February (N14.5m), April (N12m), 8 September (N10.96m) and 23 September (N10m), and what the payments that followed were for.
11. The asset manager statement for the February placement: account holder, current balance, any redemptions. Also invoices or an asset register for the April and June equipment purchases.

### Maternity and clinical services

12. Priority 1: the antenatal booking register for 2026: booking date, package, payments, expected date of delivery, place of delivery, and the reason where known.
13. What the January to March "packages" payments of N45,000 to N50,000 were.
14. For fertility: the partner laboratory agreement, the invoice for the March case, and the number of fertility cases in 2025 and 2026.

### Registration, licensing and people

15. The 2026 renewal certificate, or the committee's written status on Remita reference 1014-3323-9635, and the 2025 certificate.
16. Any committee approval or inspection report for 37 Nelson Mandela Street, Asokoro, and confirmation of whether the Jabi site is still open.
17. Licences and specialist qualifications for the four consultant obstetricians and gynaecologists, the consultant anaesthetist and the surgeon, with their sessional letters, and the name of the maternity lead.
18. The pharmacist arrangement, if any, with the pharmacist's licence; the licence of the laboratory scientist on the staff list; licences for the locum nurses and nurse anaesthetist; and any midwifery registrations.
19. The current professional indemnity policy.

### Insurers

20. A claims list by insurer: submitted, approved, paid and outstanding, January to October 2026.
21. The countersigned Allianz Partners agreement with its annexes; the Reliance tariff; and the signed agreements or provider letters for Leadway, AXA Mansard, Clearline, Henner and ECOWAS.
