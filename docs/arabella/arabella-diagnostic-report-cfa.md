## 1. The answer in one page

- Revenue: N48.7m taken January to September 2026, about N5.4m a month, from about 26 paying patients a month. You and Tolu both put it at N5m to N8m, so the baseline is agreed.
- Where it comes from: laboratory 30%, consultations 22%, fertility 12%. Maternity packages are 6%.
- Constraint: conversion and cost. Patients arrive and do not stay: 7 in 10 pay once, and 7 of the year's 11 maternity bookings delivered elsewhere.
- Cost: on our reading of the expense sheets, January to July operating cost of about N40.0m ran slightly above revenue of N37.4m, while N24.8m went into the Asokoro renovation.
- Compliance: the Asokoro premises needs its own facility registration, the 2026 renewal needs to be granted, and pharmacy sales need a pharmacist and a premises licence.
- People: a team that is proud of its care and wants more trust to decide. Everything runs through you and the Head of Nursing, which you, Tolu and your staff each named on their own.
- Recommendation: put the registration right now, fix conversion and cost over the next quarter, and expand into maternity, surgery and fertility only once those hold.

## 2. Your three questions

### Needs immediate attention

- Facility registration: register the Asokoro premises in Arabella's name, and get the 2026 renewal decision in writing. Detail in section 5.
- Pharmacy: appoint a superintendent pharmacist and apply for the pharmacy premises licence, or stop dispensing until both are in place.
- Cash: neither First Bank account held a working balance on 2 October. Set a minimum cash floor and a weekly cash report before any further capital spend.
- Front desk confidentiality: patient cases are discussed where waiting patients can hear. Move those conversations out of the waiting area this week.

### Strengthen before expanding

- Maternity conversion: find out why 7 of 11 booked mothers delivered elsewhere, and fix that before marketing more packages.
- Follow-up: book the next appointment before the patient leaves. 72% of patients paid once this year.
- Cost base: two salary rolls ran together from July. Merge the Tabitha and Arabella cost base into one, with one budget.
- Delegation: give named people authority to decide, with clear limits, so that the practice does not wait for you.
- Insurer income: chase what HMOs owe and put each panel on a current tariff. Insurers paid under 2% of receipts.
- Patient contact data: capture a mobile number at every visit. Only 14% of records have one.

### Can wait

- Building an in-house fertility unit. Today fertility is referred to a partner laboratory at a 42% margin. Keep that until volume justifies a unit.
- Expanding surgery and large-scale marketing. Both depend on conversion and registration being settled first.
- Contacting the full patient list for marketing. First tell patients about the change of name and give them an easy way to opt out.

## 6. People and leadership

- Staff survey: 13 of about 25 staff answered. Average score 4.07 out of 5.
- Safety: 9 rate patient safety excellent and 3 very good. 12 of 13 would be happy for their own family to be cared for at Arabella.
- Strongest scores: sterility and sterilisation records 5.0, kindness to patients 4.8, medicines in date 4.7.
- Weakest scores: questioning a senior decision without it being held against you 3.1, being afraid to ask questions 3.0. In a maternity unit this matters most in an emergency.
- Founder dependency: you wrote that you want the business to function without everything depending on you. Tolu and a member of staff said the same independently.
- Start time: the day is meant to begin at 8am and usually begins nearer 9am.
- Your two leadership answers differ on the constraint: you see cash to invest and a 12-month target above N60m; Tolu sees patient demand and a target of N25m to N40m. The records support neither fully. They point to conversion and cost, which is the conversation to have together.
