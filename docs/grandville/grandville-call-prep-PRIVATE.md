# Grandville, call prep. PRIVATE. Not to be sent.

Prospect: **Grandville Medical Group**, founded June 2013 by Dr Rasheed Ayobami
Aranmolate, plastic and reconstructive surgeon. Three centres: Lekki (Eleganza, by
VGC, the head office), Surulere (76 Femi Ayantuga), and Maitama, Abuja. Began as a
plastic surgery and laser practice and has pushed outward into trauma and critical
care, diagnostics, imaging, laboratory, dermatology and obstetrics. A Grandville Med
Spa sub brand exists. Smile Train cleft work. Stated intention to reach all six
geopolitical zones, and a trauma and critical care facility built with federal
financing support.

**Cold. No relationship, no referrer.** The room is a managing director or chief
executive layer plus the marketing lead. The founder is not in it.

Client-facing: `grandville-marketing-capability-deck-cfa.pdf` (18pp landscape, built
by `scripts/build-grandville-deck.py`, also copied to `docs/Grandville Medical Group -
Marketing and Growth (Deck).pdf`) and `grandville-marketing-discovery-cfa.pdf` (130
questions, built by `scripts/build-grandville-docs.py`). The questionnaire is a
**leave-behind for after the call**, not a document to open with.

Sources are public only: gml.com.ng, grandville.com.ng, and a Nairametrics piece of 3
June 2026 in which Dr Aranmolate talks about "building healthcare systems that can
compete globally while serving local realities". Nothing in either client document
claims knowledge we do not have, and slide two says so explicitly. Keep it that way
on the call, because the fastest way to lose a cold room is to be caught asserting
something about their business that is not true.

## The thesis, in one line

A group that began as one surgeon's name and now runs trauma bays and scanners is
carrying two marketing businesses that need opposite strategies, and it cannot take
the founder's name into six zones.

## The read behind the read

They took a meeting with a firm they do not know, on marketing, after thirteen years
of growing without one. That usually means one of three things, and they lead
somewhere different.

The first is that **the acute and diagnostic side is not filling**. They built trauma,
intensive care and imaging, those assets are expensive and idle, and nobody arrives
for them the way patients arrive for aesthetics. If this is it, the work is referrer
engine plus payer and employer contracts, and it is the most commercially serious
version of the conversation.

The second is **the expansion**. Six zones is in the press. You cannot franchise a
surgeon, and somebody internally has worked out that the brand will not travel. If
this is it, the work is brand architecture first, and it is urgent in a way they may
not yet feel.

The third, and the most likely given who is in the room, is that **the marketing lead
asked for help and the MD agreed to look**. In that case we are partly being
evaluated by the person we would be working alongside, and a pitch that implies their
work has been inadequate loses the room no matter how good the analysis is.

Questions 14.1 and 13.3 in the discovery set go at the first two directly. Ask 14.1
early in the call, not at the end.

## What to lead with

**Lead with the two businesses.** It is the observation that is obviously true the
moment it is said, it is flattering rather than critical, and it reframes marketing
as an architecture problem rather than a spending problem. An elective aesthetic
patient chooses deliberately over months and will fly. A trauma patient is delivered
by whoever is nearest. One brand cannot promise both, and their own med spa sub brand
shows they have already half felt it.

Then widen to the acute side filling, which is where the money is and where they are
most likely to be hurting.

Then, and only once they are nodding, the founder brand question.

**Do not open with the founder brand point.** It is the sharpest thing we have and it
is also the thing most likely to be heard as "your founder is a liability", relayed
to him badly by somebody who was not in the room. Frame it the way the deck does: his
name is the most valuable asset in the group, it simply cannot be in six places, and
the question is about their plan rather than their brand.

**Do not pitch past the questions.** The deck is eighteen slides and most of them
exist so the room knows who is asking. The slides that matter are 5, 6, 8 and 16. If
the conversation gets going on slide 5, abandon the rest and run the questions. A
cold first call that ends with them having talked for forty minutes is a win; one
where we presented for forty minutes is not.

## The marketing lead is the one to win

Two buyers, opposite fears. The MD wants to know whether this moves revenue and
whether we are safe near the brand. The marketing lead wants to know whether we are
here to take their job and whether we will make them look bad in front of the MD.

Slide 11 exists entirely for the second one. Say it out loud as well as showing it:
our default mode is augmentation, the lead keeps the brief and the accountability,
and the first two things we build are the asset library and the measurement, both of
which make **their** monthly report defensible. Never use the phrase "what is missing
today". Ask instead what they have been asking for and not getting, which is usually
budget, clinician time for content, and a consent process for before and after
photographs. Then agree with them in front of their MD.

If they have an incumbent agency, do not attack it. Ask what it does well. The gap we
fill is the part no agency can do, which is the arithmetic.

## Where the money is, in order

1. **The referrer engine for the acute and diagnostic side.** Nobody in this market
   runs one properly, it is cheap to build, and the question "which referrers used to
   send you patients and have stopped" (7.6) tends to land hard because nobody has
   ever asked them.
2. **The enquiry front door across three sites.** Section 6 exists to find out whether
   they lose patients they have already paid to attract. They will not know the
   conversion number. Not knowing it is the finding.
3. **The inbound surgical product.** They already have airport pickup and an aftercare
   home, which are the hard parts, and they have intensive care behind the theatre,
   which a Lekki day clinic cannot claim. What they do not have is a packaged pathway
   with a price and a written answer on complications after the flight. Keep the
   diaspora distinction in your pocket: the United States case is price, the United
   Kingdom case is rationing, age limits and waiting, and selling to them as one
   audience is the standard mistake.
4. **Brand architecture**, which is the biggest piece of work and the one they are
   least likely to buy first.

## Verify before the call

- **The Abuja site.** Addresses differ across listings: 5b Lephalala Close off Ganges
  Street, off Alvan Ikoku Way, is the one that appears most often, and one directory
  still has a Surulere head office. Confirm Lekki is the head office before saying so.
  Getting a site wrong in the first ten minutes of a cold call is expensive.
- **Who is actually coming.** Ask for names and roles when the meeting is confirmed.
  If the founder joins unannounced, slide 6 changes from an observation to a
  conversation about him, and it should then be raised only if he raises it.
- **Whether the trauma centre is open or announced.** The federal financing support is
  reported. Whether the facility is operating changes whether the acute side is a
  filling problem or a building problem.
- **The med spa.** Whether it is a live separate brand, a room, or a dormant name.
  Slide 5's closing line rests on it.
- **Any live reputational issue.** Check Instagram comments and the Nigerian blogs
  before the call. Aesthetic surgery in Lagos carries a particular kind of public
  complaint, and walking into it unprepared would be bad. Question 9.12 asks them
  directly, but ask it only after they trust us.

## Do not

Do not name another client, including the ones they would recognise. Slide 15 shows
five engagements with nobody named, and that restraint is itself the pitch: it is the
answer to the question they are actually asking, which is what we would say about
them to the next hospital.

Do not promise a number. Nothing on attribution, nothing on volume, nothing on
return, not even a range, until sections 3, 4 and 6 come back.

Do not mention our own products or platforms. Describe what is done.

## After the call

Send within twenty four hours: a short note of what we heard, the discovery set, and
the deck. Nothing else. The written read of their growth constraint is promised at
the end of the questionnaire and is the thing that earns the second meeting, so it
has to be genuinely useful and it has to be free.

The ask for the working session is specific: whoever owns the numbers in the room,
which is finance plus whoever runs each site, not the marketing lead alone.
