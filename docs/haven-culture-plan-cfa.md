# The Cultural Transformation Plan
**Nineteen people, twelve weeks, one behaviour at a time**
Prepared by: Consult for Africa
Project lead: Claude, Consult for Africa
Supported by: Abigail Oladejo and Dr Omotanwa Gbadebo
Date: 9 October 2026
Circulation: Internal to Consult for Africa. Not for the client.

---

## 1. Where we actually are

The town hall ran on 6 October and the team left oriented. Nineteen staff were emailed the same afternoon with the staff page, the pack and the deck. Three days on, the numbers are these.

::: stats
2 of 19 | have signed in | Sandra Okorie and Benedict Ijehon
3 | things submitted | one near miss, one pulse, one what-is-broken
0 | tasks completed | of ten seeded, across three days
:::

Two of nineteen is eleven per cent. It would be comfortable to call that a slow start. It is not: it is the number that tells us the design was wrong, and it is far more useful now than it would have been in January.

### Two structural faults, and they are ours

A task cannot be ticked. Ten tasks were seeded and the morning email lists them, and there is no endpoint and no button anywhere that marks one done. We asked nineteen people to do things and gave them no way to say they had.

Nothing ever raises a flag. The table exists and is read, and nothing writes to it. So nothing was chased, and not doing a task cost exactly nothing.

Those two together explain the zero completely. No behavioural insight is needed for it and none should be claimed.

## 2. Why the other seventeen did not come back

Behaviour needs three things present at once: the capability to act, the opportunity to act, and the motivation to. Where a behaviour is absent, at least one is missing, and the useful question is which. Taking them in turn against what we know.

| | Where Haven stands |
|---|---|
| **Capability** | Mostly fine. They have phones, they received the email, and the sign-in is a six-digit code with no password. The gap is narrow: nobody has been shown the page by a person, and watching somebody do a thing once is worth more than any instruction we write. |
| **Opportunity** | Weaker than we thought. The code goes by email, and most of the nineteen are on personal addresses they read at home rather than on shift. Text messaging is built and sits behind one unset key. After the first email nothing prompted anybody again. |
| **Motivation** | The real gap. Nothing on the page needed doing, nothing happened if they ignored it, and nothing visibly changed because anybody used it. We asked for behaviour and supplied no consequence in either direction. |

The honest reading is that we built an invitation and called it a programme. A hospital of nineteen people, mid-shift, will not return to a page out of goodwill after the first week.

## 3. What we change, in three moves

### Capability: make it possible, in person

Abigail and Dr Tanwa sit with each person individually, on shift, and watch them sign in once. Nineteen conversations of four minutes. This is not training and it is not announced as such; it is standing next to somebody while they do it once, which is the single highest-yield hour in this entire plan and the one most likely to be skipped as beneath us.

### Opportunity: put it where they already are

The sign-in code moves to text message, which needs one environment key and is otherwise built. Text reaches a nurse on shift; personal email reaches her at home on Sunday.

Then the daily drip, covered in section 5. One message, one instruction, no digest.

### Motivation: make something happen either way

Three things, in order of effect.

Close the loop out loud. The near miss already reported gets acted on this week, and what changed because of it is published to all nineteen with the reporter's permission or anonymously. One real example of somebody speaking and the hospital moving is worth more than every paragraph we have written about psychological safety.

Make the absence visible, gently. Flags start being raised. Not as a sanction: the first level is a question to the person, the second a quiet word from their line manager. The point is that not doing a thing stops being free.

Let people finish something. Completion is motivating and a list is not. Every person gets a short, visible run of things that can actually be ticked, and sees how many are left.

## 4. The sprints

Two weeks each, because that is the rhythm of the peer sessions and because a twelve-week programme with no internal joints becomes a twelve-week programme that slips once.

Each sprint targets **one behaviour**. Not three. The temptation is to run them in parallel and the result is that none of them sticks.

| Sprint | Weeks | The one behaviour | Done means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13 to 24 Oct | Everybody signs in and completes one thing | 15 of 19 signed in, each having ticked at least one task |
| 2 | 27 Oct to 7 Nov | The daily task becomes routine | Half of all seeded daily tasks ticked across the week, two weeks running |
| 3 | 10 to 21 Nov | Speaking up is normal | Six or more reports in the fortnight, and every one closed out visibly |
| 4 | 24 Nov to 5 Dec | Protocols: acknowledge and study | Every person has acknowledged every protocol that applies to them |
| 5 | 8 to 19 Dec | Protocols: tested | Eighty per cent passed on the protocols trained so far |
| 6 | 5 to 16 Jan | It runs without us | A week where the cadence holds with no chasing from CFA |

Sprint 6 falls after the season peak on purpose. Nothing new is introduced between 20 December and 4 January: the hospital is at its busiest, and a process introduced in that window will be blamed for everything that goes wrong in it.

## 5. The daily drip

One message a day, to one person, containing **one instruction**. Not a summary of outstanding items, which is a digest and gets archived.

| | |
|---|---|
| When | 07:00 Lagos, after the night shift and before the day one |
| What | The single most important thing waiting on that person, and nothing else |
| If nothing is waiting | Nothing is sent. This is what keeps the rest worth opening |
| Where it lands | Straight on the thing, never the front page |
| Friday | The exception: what changed this week because people spoke up |

The existing morning email already computes the right list and already sends nothing when the list is empty. It needs narrowing from six items to one, and the Friday variant building.

---

## 6. Buddy pairs

Everybody is paired, and the pairings are published. Pairs meet for fifteen minutes a week, on shift rather than after it.

Four rules, each of them a way this normally fails.

Pair across shifts, so the pair is a line of communication the rota does not already provide. Pair across functions where sensible, because a nurse and a front desk colleague understanding each other's constraints fixes more friction than either being trained again. Never pair somebody with the person who writes their appraisal. And rotate every quarter, so the network ends up dense rather than becoming six fixed friendships.

The pair has one job at a time, tied to the current sprint, so it has a reason to meet. In sprint 1 that job is simply: have you both signed in.

## 8. How we will know

Four numbers, reported weekly, and the first is the only one that matters until it moves.

| | Now | Sprint 1 | End of plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signed in at least once | 2 of 19 | 15 | 19 |
| Used it in the last seven days | 2 | 10 | 14 |
| Daily tasks ticked, share of those seeded | 0% | 20% | 60% |
| Reports in the fortnight | 3 | 4 | 8 or more |

The last one is the only measure here where a rise is unambiguously good and will look like bad news to anybody reading it cold. The board needs telling that before the first report, not after.

---

## 9. What would make this fail

Four things, named so they can be watched for.

We build more features instead of chasing nineteen people. The build is mostly done; the work now is human and it is duller.

The daily drip becomes a digest. The moment it carries six items it is archived unread, and the one that mattered goes with it.

A flag is used as a reprimand. One investigation that feels like a hunt costs a year of reports, and we will not get the reporting culture back inside this engagement.

We introduce something new in late December. The season is when the hospital is least able to absorb change and most likely to blame whatever is newest.
