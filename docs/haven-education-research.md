# Research memo: education as the lever for the Haven turnaround

- To: Founding Partner, CFA
- Re: Evidence and design for a world-class education system at Haven Paediatric Centre
- Date: 3 November 2026
- Status: Research draft for internal design use. Figures marked "verify" come from secondary sources and need checking against the primary paper or regulator before they go into any client document.

## Answer first

- Education can carry the turnaround only if it changes what people do on shift. Knowledge alone does not move revenue, safety or NICU volume. The evidence is clear that the transfer from course to ward depends on the work environment more than the course.
- The current build (read, quiz at 80%, clinician approval) is a sound base for knowledge. It is the weakest of the proven methods for skills. Three additions carry most of the evidence: spaced retrieval on the phone, short frequent hands-on drills in the unit, and observed sign-off on the job.
- OKRs should stop at team level. Founders and unit leads set and negotiate them (Hoshin catchball). Frontline staff see their team's two or three key results in the tasks and scoreboard they already use. No personal OKRs for frontline staff, and no link to pay.
- Do not pay for completion. Expected, completion-contingent cash rewards lower intrinsic motivation, and the African per diem record shows what happens to attendance when training becomes income. Use recognition, CPD credit, a visible competency ladder, and paid data as an enabler.
- Proposed architecture: four role tiers (who) by five curriculum strands (what) by four delivery modes (how), measured on five levels (participation, learning, behaviour, results, ROI). Each person, module and measure sits in exactly one cell.

## (a) The 10 findings that should shape design

Strength key: **Strong** = meta-analysis or several RCTs. **Moderate** = RCTs in a similar setting, or consistent controlled studies. **Weak** = case studies, self-reported data, vendor or secondary sources.

### 1. Testing beats rereading, and spacing makes it stick
- Fact: Practice testing outperforms restudy by about half a standard deviation (Rowland 2014, g = 0.50; g = 0.69 when retention is tested after more than a day). Adesope et al. 2017 report g = 0.61 to 0.70 across 272 effect sizes, and g = 0.82 for a gap of one to six days between test and final exam.
- Fact: Spaced emailed case questions raised medical students' end-of-year scores (Kerfoot 2007 RCT, p < 0.001). A 2024 meta-analysis of spaced digital education for health professionals found the effect positive but mixed at three months to two years.
- Fact: Spaced education with retrieval practice doubled the odds of evidence-based fluid prescribing in a cluster crossover trial of 354 clinicians.
- Design implication: Each Haven module should not end at one quiz. Re-serve two or three items from every passed module at about 2 days, 2 weeks and 2 months, inside the daily task list. Track the delayed score, not the first-attempt pass.
- Strength: Strong for knowledge retention. Moderate for behaviour change in clinicians.
- Sources: [Rowland and Adesope summaries, WSU](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/); [Kerfoot 2007](https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2929.2006.02644.x); [JMIR 2024 spaced digital education meta-analysis](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e57760); [Spaced education and prescribing, cluster crossover](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9327570/)

### 2. Hands-on skills decay within months unless drilled little and often
- Fact: After Helping Babies Breathe training in Tanzania, the pass rate on a skills check fell from 87.1% straight after training to 55.8% at six months.
- Fact: Low-dose, high-frequency (LDHF) practice in eight Tanzanian hospitals was associated with a 47% fall in early neonatal mortality (13.4 to 7.1 per 1,000) and a fall in fresh stillbirths. A five-year follow-up tied continued improvement to continued frequent practice.
- Fact: Jhpiego's LDHF cluster RCT in 40 Ghanaian hospitals reported a sustained decrease in facility newborn mortality and skills 28% to 31% above baseline after one year. A companion analysis found it 100% likely to be cost-effective at Ghana's GNI per capita.
- Fact: The Nigerian LDHF plus mobile mentoring RCT (Ebonyi and Kogi, 60 facilities, 299 workers) found knowledge scores equally high in both arms at 3 and 12 months. The benefit of LDHF is in skills and outcomes, not in knowledge tests.
- Design implication: NICU and resuscitation competencies need 5 to 15 minute drills on the unit, weekly or fortnightly, run by a peer practice coordinator. A quiz cannot certify a resuscitation skill.
- Strength: Strong for skill decay and LDHF in sub-Saharan Africa. Mortality figures are from before-after and cluster designs (Moderate).
- Sources: [Tanzania HBB five-year study, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0240520); [Original Tanzania HBB study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234824649_Newborn_Mortality_and_Fresh_Stillbirth_Rates_in_Tanzania_After_Helping_Babies_Breathe_Training); [Ghana LDHF cluster RCT](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5863807/); [Ghana LDHF cost-effectiveness](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12992-017-0313-x); [Nigeria LDHF RCT, BMC Health Serv Res 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7318405/)

### 3. Simulation produces large skill gains and finds hazards in the unit itself
- Fact: Technology-enhanced simulation versus no intervention: pooled effect 1.20 for knowledge, 1.09 to 1.18 for skills, about 0.80 for behaviours and 0.50 for direct patient effects (Cook et al., JAMA 2011, 609 studies). Heterogeneity was high. A 2023 update limited to RCTs against traditional teaching found 0.80.
- Fact: Simulation with deliberate practice beat traditional clinical education with an effect of 0.71 (McGaghie et al. 2011, 14 studies).
- Fact: In situ NICU simulation surfaces latent safety threats. Cincinnati found 29 in situ, leading to 19 unit changes. A nine-hospital Austrian programme found 400 and cut equipment and environment threats by 62% between rounds, which rose again when the programme paused.
- Design implication: Monthly in situ NICU drills on the real resuscitaire, with a two-minute debrief that logs any equipment or process gap straight into the safety register. This links education to the just culture register already built.
- Strength: Strong for skills. Moderate for patient outcomes. Moderate for latent threat detection.
- Sources: [Cook et al. JAMA 2011, full text](https://med.virginia.edu/medical-simulation-center/wp-content/uploads/sites/254/2016/01/2011JAMA_Cook_Meta-analysis.pdf); [2023 RCT-only update, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1149048/full); [McGaghie 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3102783/); [Cincinnati NICU latent threats](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/study-nicu-makes-19-improvements-based-on-latent-safety-threats/); [Austrian multicentre in situ study](https://laerdal.com/solutions/healthcare-quality-improvement/resources/3-studies-showing-how-simulation-improves-care-quality-in-the-nicu/)

### 4. Whether training is used on shift depends on the work environment, not the course
- Fact: Across 89 studies, transfer of training was predicted by motivation and a supportive work environment, as well as ability (Blume, Ford, Baldwin and Huang 2010). Environment mattered more for open skills (judgement, leadership, communication) than for closed skills (software steps).
- Fact: The WHO's 2019 digital health guideline (Recommendation 10) recommends mobile learning only on condition that it complements, not replaces, traditional in-service training. Its moderate-confidence finding: health workers struggle with mobile tools their clinical mentors do not also use.
- Design implication: Every module needs a named supervisor behaviour attached (for example, the nurse lead checks two charts for the new step in the next week). Unit leads and the CMD must use the app visibly, or staff will treat it as optional.
- Strength: Strong (Blume meta-analysis). Moderate (WHO guideline).
- Sources: [Blume et al. 2010, Journal of Management](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2165041900); [WHO guideline, evidence and recommendations](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK541898/)

### 5. System training works when it is short, specific to the role's workflow, and backed by proactive super-users
- Fact: In the KLAS Arch Collaborative, clinicians who strongly agreed their initial EHR training prepared them scored 90 points higher on the Net EHR Experience Score than those who strongly disagreed. Workflow-specific training correlated with higher satisfaction. Gains flattened beyond 30 to 60 minutes per session and beyond 3 to 5 hours a year of ongoing training.
- Fact: Children's Health (Dallas) raised nurse EHR satisfaction 14.5 points by combining education, shared ownership and personalisation.
- Fact: A Yale mixed-methods study found the unit whose super-users were proactive and explained the reasons behind steps saw greater gains in informatics proficiency (p = 0.03).
- Design implication: Build a role-by-system matrix (see part b). Train each role only on the screens it uses, in sessions under 30 minutes. Appoint one super-user per system who is expected to approach people, not wait to be asked.
- Strength: Moderate (large observational dataset, self-reported, correlational). Weak to Moderate for super-users.
- Sources: [KLAS Clinician Training report](https://klasresearch.com/archcollaborative/report/clinician-training/303); [KLAS Clinician Training 2023](https://klasresearch.com/archcollaborative/report/clinician-training-2023/480); [KLAS case studies](https://klasresearch.com/archcollaborative/casestudies); [Yale super-user study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4407776/)

### 6. Specific hard goals lift performance, less so on complex work, and they have side effects
- Fact: Specific, difficult goals outperform "do your best" with effect sizes of about d = 0.42 to 0.80 (Locke and Latham 2002, summarising their meta-analyses). On the most complex tasks the effect drops to about 0.41 (Wood, Mento and Locke 1987).
- Fact: Ordóñez et al. (2009) document the side effects of over-prescribed goals: narrowed focus on what is measured, riskier choices, unethical behaviour (Sears mechanics overcharging to hit quotas), inhibited learning, and eroded intrinsic motivation.
- Design implication: Clinical work in a NICU is complex. Set learning goals ("master the new feed protocol") for complex clinical tasks and performance goals ("100% of HMO patients have an authorisation code before admission") for routine tasks. Pair every revenue key result with a quality or safety guardrail so staff cannot hit one by harming the other.
- Strength: Strong (goal setting). Moderate (side effects, a review of cases).
- Sources: [Locke and Latham 2002](https://med.stanford.edu/content/dam/sm/s-spire/documents/PD.locke-and-latham-retrospective_Paper.pdf); [Goals Gone Wild, Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/13591.pdf)

### 7. Strategy deployment works when the priorities are few, negotiated, and kept apart from pay
- Fact: ThedaCare ran True North in four categories (safety and quality, people, delivery, cost and productivity) and targeted at least 50% improvement on breakthrough measures. Its former CEO John Toussaint said the Lean conversion would have moved far faster had strategy deployment started at the beginning.
- Fact: The recurring failure modes of Hoshin Kanri are too many breakthrough objectives (Toyota's president picks 4 to 6 a year), catchball run as a single pro-forma meeting, targets cascaded to every individual, and reviews that become status reports.
- Fact: Doerr separates committed OKRs (expected at 100%) from aspirational ones (expected at 60% to 70%), and argues OKRs and bonuses should be kept separate to stop sandbagging.
- Design implication: Haven gets one True North (four headings), at most three quarterly objectives, and a catchball round of two to three exchanges between founders and unit leads before anything is final.
- Strength: Weak to Moderate (practitioner and case evidence; no controlled trials of OKRs in hospitals).
- Sources: [ThedaCare and strategy deployment, AQuA case study](https://aqua.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/A-Sense-of-Urgency-A-Sense-of-Hope1-Final.pdf); [Hoshin Kanri failure modes](https://iaec.online/hoshin-kanri-strategy-deployment-failure-modes/); [Why Hoshin Kanri does not work, Art of Lean](https://artoflean.com/articles/why-does-hoshin-kanri-not-work/); [Doerr on committed and aspirational OKRs](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2019/03/02/measure-what-matters/)

### 8. Paying for completion backfires; recognition and feedback work
- Fact: Across 128 experiments, expected tangible rewards lowered intrinsic motivation (completion-contingent d = -0.36, engagement-contingent d = -0.40, performance-contingent d = -0.28). Unexpected tangible rewards had no effect (d = 0.01). Positive verbal feedback raised it (d = 0.33) (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999).
- Fact: Across 183 studies and 212,468 people, intrinsic motivation predicted performance (rho = 0.21 to 0.45) with or without incentives. Incentives predicted quantity of work better; intrinsic motivation predicted quality better. Directly performance-tied incentives crowded out intrinsic motivation (Cerasoli, Nicklin and Ford 2014).
- Fact: Unannounced public recognition raised subsequent performance in a 300-worker field experiment (Bradler et al. 2016).
- Fact: In African health systems, training per diems have produced "ghost participants", workshop jumping, and workshops held for the allowance. In Burkina Faso, per diem income exceeded salaries in two districts.
- Design implication: No cash per module. Use recognition at huddles, unannounced thanks from founders, CPD credit, and a competency ladder where pay steps follow observed competence, not completed modules.
- Strength: Strong (two large meta-analyses). Moderate (field experiment). Moderate (per diem evidence, mostly qualitative but consistent).
- Sources: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf); [Cerasoli et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24491020/); [Bradler et al. 2016, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2669); [Per diems in the health sector, Health Policy and Planning](https://academic.oup.com/heapol/article/28/3/237/551257); [Per diem scoping review, Globalization and Health](https://globalizationandhealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12992-020-00571-6)

### 9. Team tools improve culture reliably; hard outcomes need deliberate measurement
- Fact: TeamSTEPPS consistently improves teamwork perceptions and safety culture (a large effect in newly graduated nurses, eta squared at or above 0.14). Reviews find patient outcome evidence thin and slow to appear. A surgical ward saw culture gains at 6 months and organisational outcomes by 12 months.
- Fact: A BMJ Quality and Safety review of 24 huddle studies found 11 of the unit-based studies reporting full statistics showed significant improvement in some or all outcomes. Evidence for hospital-wide and tiered huddles is at an early stage.
- Fact: The Moraros 2016 review of Lean in healthcare found no significant link with patient satisfaction or health outcomes and a negative association with worker satisfaction, with inconsistent process gains. A 2020 review found Lean reduced waiting time and length of stay in ambulatory care.
- Design implication: Adopt the specific tools (SBAR handover, a 10-minute daily huddle, the two-challenge rule, standard work for the top ten tasks) without branding a "Lean programme". Measure the hard outcomes from day one.
- Strength: Moderate for culture. Weak for patient outcomes.
- Sources: [TeamSTEPPS scoping review 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2039-4403/16/3/79); [TeamSTEPPS in newly graduated nurses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10935822/); [TeamSTEPPS surgical ward](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7856763/); [Huddles systematic review, BMJ Qual Saf 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32265256); [Moraros et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4833201); [Lean and patient flow 2020](https://www.ispor.org/publications/journals/value-in-health/abstract/Volume-23--Issue-2/Effects-of-Lean-Healthcare-on-Patient-Flow--A-Systematic-Review)

### 10. Phone-based learning works in this region when it is a reference on shift and is paired with people
- Fact: The Safe Delivery App cluster RCT in Ethiopia (70 to 73 facilities) roughly doubled midwives' skills in neonatal resuscitation and postpartum haemorrhage, sustained at 12 months. Workers used it to revise and recall procedures on the job.
- Fact: Project ECHO in Namibia, Zambia and paediatric oncology nursing across 12 African countries raised knowledge and confidence. Zambia linked adherence to ECHO advice with better HIV outcomes. Nurse participation in Tanzania was only about 5%, so attendance cannot be assumed.
- Fact: Moderated WhatsApp groups supported learning among newly graduated nurses in Oyo State (randomised by school) and nurse tutors (1,530 posts in six weeks).
- Design implication: Every module should also exist as a one-screen job aid the nurse can open at the cot side. Run a moderated case discussion (ECHO style, 30 minutes, fortnightly) with an external paediatric or neonatal specialist.
- Strength: Moderate (one strong RCT plus consistent programme evaluations).
- Sources: [Safe Delivery App RCT](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12978-023-01635-7); [Safe Delivery App qualitative study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12978-019-0725-6); [ECHO systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6854140/); [Zambia ECHO evaluation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12960-023-00806-8); [Tanzania ECHO nurse participation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12360461/); [WhatsApp and Nigerian nurse graduates](https://theconversation.com/how-whatsapp-groups-support-nigerias-nurse-graduates-124514); [Nurse tutor WhatsApp study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30627340)

### Supporting evidence on the operating link (revenue and retention)
- Revenue capture: HFMA estimates up to 1% of net charges are lost to charge leakage in US hospitals; vendor figures of 3% to 5% are unverified. The best documented recovery (Novant, $7.5m in 15 months) bundled area-specific training with new tools and dashboards, so training alone cannot be credited. Haven's leakage is likely higher than US levels given manual billing; this needs a baseline audit. Strength: Weak. [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/revenue-cycle/charge-capture/55358/)
- Retention: Reviews list career development as one of five themes in nurse retention, and one-year residency and mentoring programmes improve retention of new graduates. A direct effect of general training on turnover is not established. Strength: Moderate for structured residency and mentoring; Weak for training in general. [Nurse retention review](https://ejournal.lucp.net/index.php/mjn/article/view/5234); [Intent to stay review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10077373/)
- Interleaving: mixing similar categories in practice gives g = 0.42 overall, larger for complex, visually similar material (Brunmair and Richter 2019). Useful for mixing similar-looking cases (sepsis versus normal variation; HMO plan types), not for unrelated topics. Strength: Strong. [Brunmair and Richter 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/)
- Microlearning: positive for knowledge and confidence in health professions students, with short follow-up and a thin evidence base for practising nurses. Strength: Weak to Moderate. [JMIR scoping review](https://mededu.jmir.org/2019/2/e13997/)

## (b) Proposed MECE architecture: the Haven Learning System

The system has four dimensions. Every person sits in one tier and one role. Every module sits in one strand and one mode. Every measure sits on one level.

### 1. Who: four tiers, one role profile per job

| Tier | Members | Defining test |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Governance | Five founders (the CMD sits here for board work) | Sets direction and owns the True North |
| T2 Unit leads | NICU lead, nursing lead, pharmacy, lab, billing and HMO, customer service, admin lead; CMD as clinical lead | Owns a team's key results and signs off its competencies |
| T3 Clinical frontline | Medical officers, nurses, health attendants, pharmacy and lab staff | Touches the child or the child's medicines and samples |
| T4 Non-clinical frontline | Customer service, HMO and billing officers, admin | Touches the family, the payer or the record, not the child |

- Rule: a person with two jobs (for example the CMD) has one profile per job, and each profile sits in one tier. The profile lists the strands, systems and competencies that job requires.
- Embedded CFA consultant: not a tier. Acts as programme owner and faculty.

### 2. What: five curriculum strands

| Strand | Scope (what is in) | Boundary (what is out) |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Direction | True North, quarterly objectives, team key results, how my tasks move them, reading the scoreboard | How to perform the tasks themselves |
| S2 Clinical care | Haven SOPs, NICU and paediatric competencies, IPC, medicines safety, resuscitation | Software steps, billing rules |
| S3 Systems and revenue | Every system a role touches: staff app, billing and charge capture, HMO authorisation and claims, records or EMR, inventory and stock | Clinical judgement |
| S4 Teamwork and improvement | Huddles, SBAR handover, speaking up, escalation, standard work, PDSA cycles, just culture and the safety register | Content of any one SOP |
| S5 Professional essentials | Statutory and regulatory duties (HEFAMAA, licence and CPD, NDPR data protection, safeguarding, fire), conduct, communication with families | Anything already in S1 to S4 |

Role by system matrix for S3 (each cell is a module, sized under 30 minutes):

| System | T1 | T2 | T3 clinical | T4 non-clinical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Staff app (tasks, rota, flags, pulse, leave) | Founder grid reading | Assigning, approving, reviewing | Daily use | Daily use |
| Billing and charge capture | Revenue dashboard | Unit leakage review | Recording every billable item at the point of care | Invoicing, reconciliation |
| HMO authorisation and claims | Payer mix and ageing | Escalation of rejections | Documentation that supports the claim | Codes, NIN, diagnosis coding, NHIA list, submissions, appeals |
| Records or EMR | Audit access | Chart audit | Documentation standard | Registration, filing |
| Inventory and stock | Spend view | Reorder authority | Use and wastage recording | Stores, receipts |

### 3. How: four delivery modes

| Mode | Use it for | Haven format | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Phone learning | Knowledge, rules, steps | Existing read and quiz modules, plus spaced retrieval at 2 days, 2 weeks, 2 months; one-screen job aid per module | Findings 1, 10 |
| M2 Practice and simulation | Psychomotor and team skills | LDHF drills of 5 to 15 minutes on the unit; monthly in situ NICU scenario with debrief | Findings 2, 3 |
| M3 On the job | Applying it on shift | Daily 10-minute huddle, super-user floor rounds, observed sign-off by the unit lead, ECHO-style case review | Findings 4, 5, 9 |
| M4 Cohort sessions | Direction and judgement | Quarterly OKR workshop and catchball; monthly leads' learning hour; induction day | Findings 6, 7 |

Lifecycle sits inside each mode, not as a fifth mode: Induction (first 30 days), Core (role profile complete), Refresh (spaced and drilled), Advance (next rung on the ladder).

How the OKR line works at Haven (S1 delivered through M4 then M3):
- Founders set one True North with four headings: Safe care, Our people, The family's experience, Sustainable growth (N45m to N50m a month at an 18% to 20% EBITDA floor).
- Founders draft at most three quarterly objectives. Example: "Make the NICU the first call for paediatricians in Ikeja."
- Unit leads propose two or three key results for their team. Two or three rounds of catchball before sign-off. Each revenue key result carries a safety or quality guardrail.
- Frontline staff do not write OKRs. Their team's key results appear on the scoreboard, and their daily tasks are tagged to the key result they serve.
- Committed key results (for example, every HMO admission authorised before the child reaches the ward) are expected at 100%. Aspirational ones (for example, NICU occupancy) at 60% to 70%. Neither is used in pay or appraisal ratings.

### 4. How it is measured: five levels

| Level | Question | Haven measure | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Participation | Did they take part? | Modules completed on time, drill attendance, huddle attendance | Unit lead |
| L2 Learning | Do they know it and can they do it? | Delayed retrieval score at 2 weeks and 2 months; drill pass on an observed checklist | CFA consultant |
| L3 Behaviour | Do they do it on shift? | Chart audit against the SOP, items billed per discharge versus items used, authorisation before admission, handovers using SBAR | Unit lead |
| L4 Results | Did the numbers move? | NICU admissions and occupancy, revenue per patient day, HMO rejection rate and days to payment, safety events reported and harm events, staff turnover | Founders |
| L5 Return | Was it worth the cost? | ROI on the two or three largest programmes only (for example, revenue capture training: recovered revenue less programme cost) | CFA consultant |

- Rule: report L3 and L4 to founders, not L1. Completion is a hygiene measure for leads.
- Rule: the scoreboard shows teams, not ranked individuals. In a 20-person workforce a ranked list names people.

## (d) Traps to avoid

1. **Measuring completion as success.** Completion is L1. A 100% completion rate with no change in charts or billing is a failure. Report L3 and L4 to founders.
2. **Using read-and-quiz to certify skills.** The Nigerian LDHF trial shows knowledge scores look the same whichever way you train. Resuscitation, line care and feeding need observed sign-off.
3. **One-off big-bang training.** Skills fall from 87% to 56% in six months without practice. Budget for refreshers in the rota, not a launch event.
4. **Cascading OKRs to every individual.** It produces paperwork and sandbagging. Stop at team level.
5. **Tying OKRs or module completion to pay.** It invites gaming (Sears), lowers intrinsic motivation, and in Nigeria risks recreating per diem behaviour. Pay follows observed competence on the ladder.
6. **Too many priorities.** More than three quarterly objectives means no choice has been made.
7. **Catchball as theatre.** One meeting where leads are told the targets is not catchball. Leads must be able to change a key result.
8. **A revenue target without a guardrail.** Every revenue key result needs a paired safety or quality measure, or staff will hit it the wrong way.
9. **Ranked individual leaderboards.** In a 20-person team, peer comparison identifies and shames individuals. Show team results; give individual feedback privately.
10. **Linking the safety register to blame or to quiz failure.** Just culture depends on reporting being safe. Never use a safety report to assign remedial modules as punishment; route system causes to process fixes.
11. **The clinician approval bottleneck.** If every module waits for the CMD, the library stalls. Set a turnaround standard (for example 7 days) and allow a second named approver.
12. **Branding a Lean programme.** The Lean evidence on patient outcomes and staff satisfaction is mixed. Adopt the tools; skip the badge.
13. **Pushing data cost on to staff.** At N431 to N475 per GB after the January 2025 tariff rise, video-heavy modules are a pay cut. Keep modules light and pay a data allowance.
14. **Training without the work environment.** If unit leads do not ask about the new step in the next shift, transfer will not happen (Blume).

## (e) What is specific to Nigeria and Lagos

### Regulators and CPD
- MDCN (doctors): 20 CPD credit units a year are a condition of annual licence renewal (40 for a two-year renewal), from seven activity categories. MDCN's own CPD platform is optional; CPD itself is mandatory. [MDCN CPD requirements](https://cpd.portal.mdcn.gov.ng/requirements); [What you should know about MDCN CPD](https://www.mdcn.gov.ng/sub-page/education/what-you-should-know-about-mdcn-cpd-activity); [MDCN registration guideline](https://www.mdcn.gov.ng/public/storage/documents/sub_document_1215406857.pdf)
- NMCN (nurses and midwives): the licence runs on a three-year cycle. Most sources give 6 credit units per cycle, with at least 3 from an NMCN-approved Mandatory Continuing Professional Development Programme (MCPDP) workshop. NMCN publishes guidelines for private MCPDP providers. Figures conflict across sources; verify with NMCN before relying on them. [NMCN guidelines for private MCPDP providers](https://www.nmcn.gov.ng/docs/GUIDELINES%20FOR%20PRIVATE%20PROVIDERS%20OF%20MCPDP%20IN%20NIGERIA.pdf); [Renewal rejection reasons, NMCN official](https://medicalworldnigeria.com/post/Common-Reasons-for-Rejection-and-Disapproval-of-Renewal-Applications-in-Nursing-and-Midwifery-Council-of-Nigeria-by-Adigun-Rotimi-NMCN-Abuja?pid=44187)
- Implication: CPD credit is the incentive that costs Haven nothing in cash and that staff need anyway. Two options to test: (1) partner with an accredited MCPDP provider to run a Haven-hosted neonatal workshop; (2) give every completed strand a Haven certificate staff can log as self-directed CPD where the category allows. Confirm which MDCN categories accept in-house learning.
- HEFAMAA (Lagos facility regulator): requires at least one registered nurse or midwife per 8 in-patient beds per shift, and requires facility owners to verify every professional's certificates and licences with the professional body. Inspection checks staff qualifications and infection control. Implication: a licence and CPD tracker inside the staff app is both a compliance control and an education feature. [HEFAMAA private hospital requirements](https://hefamaa.lagosstate.gov.ng/registration/private-hospital/); [HEFAMAA FAQ](https://hefamaa.lagosstate.gov.ng/faq/)
- NHIA and HMOs: NHIA directed HMOs to issue authorisation codes within one hour of a provider's request (reported effective 1 April 2025); if they do not, the provider may proceed and inform NHIA. NHIA logged 1,232 complaints against HMOs in 2024, mainly delayed or denied codes and late payment. Common claim failures cited are missing NIN, missing diagnosis code, and drugs outside the NHIA list. Implication: these rules are S3 content for the HMO desk and for nurses who document. Time to authorisation and rejection reasons are L3 and L4 measures. [NHIA one-hour rule](https://thesun.ng/nhia-hmos-must-issue-codes-within-one-hour-of-request/); [NHIA sanctions and complaints](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/national/nhia-sanctions-49-healthcare-facilities-47-hmos-resolves-2929-cases/); [Claims documentation guide, secondary](https://www.easyclinic.io/nhia-accreditation-nigeria/)

### Phones, data and power
- The NCC allowed a 50% tariff rise in January 2025. 1GB moved from N287.50 to about N431 to N475. [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/consumers-pay-65-more-for-1gb-after-tariff-hike/); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1636542-ncc-increases-data-call-rates-by-50-tariff-adjustment/)
- Design rules: text and images first; any video under 2 minutes and compressed; cache modules for offline use on the hospital Wi-Fi; a fixed monthly data allowance for every staff member paid as an enabler, not tied to completion; a shared tablet at each nursing station for staff with low-end or shared phones. Power cuts argue for offline-capable job aids.
- WhatsApp is where Nigerian nurses already learn from each other (Oyo State studies). Use a moderated Haven clinical group for case questions, with a rule that no patient identifiers are posted (NDPR).

### Workforce and migration
- NMCN's registrar reported over 15,000 nurses and midwives left Nigeria in 2023. In February 2024 NMCN required two years' post-qualification experience before verifying certificates for foreign boards, which nurses challenged in court. [Nigerian Observer](https://nigerianobservernews.com/2024/02/japa-15000-nurses-left-nigeria-in-2023-says-registrar-nursing-council/); [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/japa-health-workers-kick-as-nursing-council-introduces-new-rules/)
- Implication: Haven will train some people who then leave. Design for it: (1) make the system the asset (protocols, job aids, super-user roles survive any one person); (2) treat development as a retention lever, with a visible ladder and mentoring for new graduates, which has the best retention evidence; (3) shorten time to competence in induction so a replacement is productive within 30 days. Avoid punitive training bonds, which signal distrust and are hard to enforce.

### Culture and practice
- Per diem expectations: in much of the Nigerian public sector, training means an allowance. State plainly at launch that Haven learning happens in paid time and is not an allowance event.
- Hierarchy: steep doctor-nurse and senior-junior gradients make speaking up hard. The TeamSTEPPS speaking-up tools and the two-challenge rule are worth teaching to both sides, with the CMD modelling them first. (This is a practice judgement, not a Nigeria-specific finding.)
- Local proof: the Nigerian LDHF trial (Ebonyi and Kogi) shows LDHF and mobile mentoring are feasible in Nigerian facilities, and that knowledge alone is not where the difference lies.
