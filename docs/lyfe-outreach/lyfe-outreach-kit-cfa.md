## The number, and why it is a calendar problem

Dr Kpaduwa sees patients Tuesdays and Wednesdays, 11 to 1. Four half hours a day, eight a week, about 31 between now and 3 November. Twenty is a 65% fill.

The trap is where those slots sit. Only twelve fall after AGELESS on 21 October, and the evening is the single biggest source of warm, ready-to-book demand. So the event cannot deliver the number by itself. **At least eight of the sixteen pre-event slots have to be filled from the channels below, in the next fortnight.** If the pre-event period goes quiet, twenty is gone and no amount of effort in the last ten days recovers it.

One decision changes this more than all the outreach combined. If she will sit sixteen half hours a week instead of eight, capacity goes to about 60 and twenty stops being a stretch. Ask her before you spend a naira.

## Doctors

This is the highest-trust channel in aesthetics and the one with the shortest path to a booking. A patient who arrives on a colleague's recommendation has already made the decision you spend money trying to manufacture.

Two audiences, and they need different messages. Clinicians who will **refer** are gynaecologists, dermatologists, GPs, bariatric physicians and anyone seeing women after childbirth. Clinicians who are **themselves the buyer** are the consultants and their spouses, who can afford this and who trust a credential they recognise.

**To a doctor you know, on WhatsApp.** Short, no brochure, no link until they reply.

> Good afternoon Dr ——. Chinwe Kpaduwa is in Lagos until early November. She is a plastic surgeon, board certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery and a Fellow of the American College of Surgeons, and she is seeing a small number of patients by video before she travels. If you have anyone who has been asking about body after children, breast reduction, or keloids and scarring, I would be glad to send you the details. No obligation either way.

**To a doctor you do not know, by email.** Subject: *Referrals for a board certified plastic surgeon, Lagos, to early November*.

> Dear Dr ——,
>
> I am writing because you may see patients for whom this is useful.
>
> Dr Chinwe Kpaduwa is a plastic surgeon practising in Lagos, board certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery and a Fellow of the American College of Surgeons. Both are verifiable publicly. She is in the country until early November and is taking consultations by video, thirty minutes, ₦150,000, credited in full against treatment.
>
> She sees body after childbirth, breast surgery including reduction and reconstruction, facial work, and the scarring and keloid cases that are common here and poorly served. If an operation is not the right answer, that is what she tells the patient.
>
> If you would like to refer someone, or to speak to her yourself first, the details are at consultforafrica.com/lyfe/consult?src=doctor.
>
> With kind regards,

**What not to do.** Do not offer a referral fee. Fee splitting is a professional conduct problem and the kind of doctor you want will be insulted by it. The offer is a good outcome for their patient and a colleague who writes back.

## Posting in groups

Groups punish advertising and reward usefulness. A post that reads as a flyer gets ignored, and in a professional group it gets you removed.

**In a doctors' group.** Clinical register, no selling.

> Sharing in case it is useful to anyone's patients. Dr Chinwe Kpaduwa, ABPS board certified plastic surgeon, FACS, is in Lagos until early November and taking referrals. Body contouring after childbirth, breast surgery, facial, and keloid and scar work. Video consultation, thirty minutes, credited against treatment if the patient proceeds. Details here and happy to answer questions: consultforafrica.com/lyfe/consult?src=group

**In a women's or lifestyle group.** Answer a question people are already asking rather than announce something.

> For anyone who has asked in here about surgery after children, or about whether to travel abroad for it. There is a plastic surgeon in Lagos at the moment who is board certified in the United States, which is rare here and checkable. She is doing video consultations until early November. The reason I mention the travelling question is that the surgery usually goes fine wherever you have it, and the problem is week three, when you want the person who operated on you in the same city. Details: consultforafrica.com/lyfe/consult?src=group

**Rules for whoever posts.** Never post the same wording in two groups on the same day. Never post into a group you are not genuinely a member of. Answer every reply within the hour, because the replies are the actual lead and a post with unanswered questions under it converts nobody.

## High-end salons and spas

The best aesthetic referral partner in Lagos is the person who has already been trusted with someone's appearance and has an hour of their undivided attention. A good colourist hears things a doctor never will.

**Target the owner, not the floor.** Ten to fifteen salons, spas and medispas in Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Lekki Phase 1 and Banana Island. Go in person, once, with something physical.

> I run the practice for Dr Chinwe Kpaduwa, a plastic surgeon who is board certified in the United States and is working in Lagos until early November. Your clients ask you things they would not ask a doctor. I am not asking you to sell anything or to hand out leaflets. I would like to leave you a few cards, and if anyone ever asks you where to go, you have somewhere real to send them. If they come, I will tell you they came, which is the part most people never bother to do.

**Why this works and most salon partnerships do not.** Do not ask for shelf space, do not ask them to promote anything, and do not offer commission, which turns a trusted recommendation into a sales pitch the client can smell. Ask for one thing only: that they have an answer when asked. Then close the loop, every time, so they know it was real.

**The reciprocity that costs nothing.** Offer the owner and their senior staff a consultation at no charge. They will use it, they will talk about it for a year, and it costs you a slot you would otherwise not have filled.

## Members clubs

Capital Club is already hosting AGELESS on 21 October, which is a relationship you should be using for more than the venue hire.

Ask the club for three things, in this order. **Their list for the evening**, because their members overlap your audience almost exactly and the invitation is more welcome coming from the club. **A mention in the members' newsletter or WhatsApp broadcast**, which costs them nothing and is worth more than a paid post. **A standing arrangement after the event**, where Lyfe becomes the practice the club refers to.

The others worth approaching on the same basis are Ikoyi Club, Lagos Motor Boat Club, Metropolitan Club and the better private gyms, where the membership and the buyer are the same person.

> I am hosting an evening at the Capital Club on 21 October on health, beauty and longevity, with a board certified plastic surgeon and a panel of clinicians. It is the kind of evening your members would enjoy and I would like to invite a number of them. Separately, the surgeon is seeing patients privately until early November, and if the club would ever like a name to give a member who asks, I would be glad to be that name.

## The digital campaign

Keep this small and sharp. With four weeks and a diary of 31 slots, the job is not reach, it is reaching the few hundred people in Lagos who are already deciding.

**Audiences, in order of value.** Retarget everyone who has touched the site, the event page or the consultation page, which is the cheapest conversion you will buy all month. Then a lookalike from the March list once it is uploaded. Then interest and income targeting on women 32 to 55 in Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Lekki and Banana Island. Broad awareness is a waste of this window.

**The four angles that work bottom of funnel**, each pointing at the objection rather than the procedure. The credential you can verify. Your complication happens at home, not in Istanbul. The consultation is credited back, so the only thing you risk is half an hour. And her own line, that the point is to look like yourself.

**What not to run.** No before and after imagery, no named prescription products, and no outcome claims. ARCON requires pre-exposure approval and the liability sits on the advertiser, with a ₦500,000 minimum fine. The compliant configuration is also the higher-trust one for this buyer, so nothing is being given up.

**Measurement.** One number matters, which is cost per paid consultation, not cost per lead and certainly not reach. If a channel is not producing booked half hours inside a week, stop it and move the money.
