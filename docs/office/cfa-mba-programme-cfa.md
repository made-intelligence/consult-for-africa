## Summary

- Aim: by October 2027 you can manage in healthcare: read a hospital's numbers, run a team, deal with doctors, patients and boards, and judge a commercial decision.
- Length: 12 courses and a capstone, 9 November 2026 to 29 October 2027, in four terms.
- Time: about four hours a week. Most of it is done on live work, not beside it.
- Material: a workbook for each course, sent the week before it starts, with weekly tests and exercises. The cases are CFA engagements.
- Personal: each course has a core everyone takes and a track for each of you. Ezinne's track leans to management and the executive office. Abigail's leans to patient operations and Mezo.
- Faculty: a reviewer from the firm for each course marks your weekly work and examines you. Debo reads the capstones.
- The Academy: where an Academy foundation track covers a course's basics, it is set as pre-reading. Abigail's Friday Academy hour continues.

## 2. The courses

### C1 Medicine and the Hospital

- Content: medical terminology, the body by system, hospital roles and documents, tests and procedures, how patients and money move.
- Mezo strand: in every week, matching a caller's words to the right specialist, red flags, preparing patients, HMO checks. Starts with the Care Coordinator Safety Card, which applies from today.
- Workbook: attached.

### C2 Managing Through Others

- Content: writing a brief someone else can finish without asking, choosing who and how much to delegate, check-ins and review, feedback, running a small team's week.
- Ezinne's track: tasks you write for Favour, Ruth and Abigail, and breaking Debo's work into sub-tasks.
- Abigail's track: briefing Mezo facilities and doctors, and asking colleagues for what you need.
- Workbook: attached.

### C3 Writing and Communicating for Business

- Content: the firm's house style, answer first, memos, minutes, client emails, WhatsApp to senior doctors, presenting to a room.
- Cases: rewriting real office documents to the standard.
- Pre-reading: Academy, Professional Standards.

### C4 The Nigerian Health Market and Health Economics

- Content: who pays (out of pocket, HMOs, NHIA, state schemes, corporates, diaspora), who regulates, who owns, and why each behaves as it does.
- Cases: Aman HMO's network, Haven's board, Arabella in Abuja.
- Pre-reading: Academy, Healthcare Fundamentals.

### C5 Accounting and Hospital Finance

- Content: the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow; revenue lines; costs; tariffs; payer mix; debtor days; why profitable hospitals run out of cash.
- Cases: reading a client's monthly figures, with commercial terms removed.
- Pre-reading: Academy, Financial Literacy for Healthcare.

### C6 Data, Spreadsheets and Decisions

- Content: clean data, lookups, pivot tables, a chart that makes one point, and reading a number critically.
- Cases: the platform's own pipeline and Mezo request data.

### C7 Operations: Patient Flow and Reading a Hospital

- Content: patient flow, waits and bottlenecks, capacity, theatre and bed use, quality and safety systems.
- Practicum: a structured walk through a client site or a Mezo network facility, written up as an observation note.

### C8 Marketing and Patient Acquisition

- Content: how patients choose a doctor, referral networks, pricing, digital channels, the rules on advertising medical services.
- Cases: Mezo, the aesthetics businesses, the AGELESS evening.

### C9 Strategy and the Consulting Method

- Content: structuring a problem, hypotheses, evidence, the pyramid principle, building a recommendation.
- Cases: the Haven growth strategy and the Medbury division.
- Pre-reading: Academy, Core Consulting Skills.

### C10 Leading People and Organisations

- Content: hiring, onboarding, evaluation, culture, conflict, motivation, and managing professionals who are senior to you.
- Cases: Haven's culture work, the office itself.

### C11 Negotiation and Commercial Judgement

- Content: preparing a negotiation, anchors and concessions, reading the other side, saying no, contracts in plain English.
- Cases: how the firm's own deals were shaped, without the figures.

### C12 Governance, Law and Ethics in Healthcare

- Content: boards and committees, board packs and minutes, clinical governance, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, medical ethics, conflicts of interest.
- Cases: Havana's two-family board, House of Refuge's readiness work.

### Capstone

- Ezinne: a full briefing and decision pack on a hospital Debo is about to meet: the market, the numbers, the people and a recommendation.
- Abigail: the Mezo care coordination playbook: matching, safety, payers, follow-up, built from a year of doing it.
- Defence: 45 minutes with Debo and the course reviewers.

## 3. How a course runs

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday | Read the week's section; do the drill or practice | 2 hours |
| During the week | The exercise, done on live work wherever possible | 1 hour |
| Friday | The weekly test, closed book, answers on your task | 30 minutes |

- Tasks: one per course per week on your desk, assigned by the course reviewer, due Friday.
- In November two courses run together (C1 and C2). That is the heaviest month of the year. From January it is one course at a time.

---

## 4. Assessment and the award

| Measure | Typical weight (each workbook sets its own) |
|---|---|
| Weekly tests | 30% |
| Exercises on live work | 30% |
| Course exam or practical | 40% |

- Course pass: 75%. A course not passed is retaken in the next gap between courses.
- Award: the CFA MBA certificate, issued by the firm when all twelve courses and the capstone are passed. It is an internal award, not a university degree, and the record behind it is what counts.
- Progression: results feed your monthly evaluation under Learning, and the capstone counts as evidence toward the firm's first consultant tier.

## 5. What we ask of you

- Protect the Tuesday block. Move it if you must; do not drop it.
- Use it at work the same week. A term used correctly in a minute, or a brief that worked first time, is worth more than a test score.
- Ask the reviewer when stuck, as a comment on the week's task.
- Learn together. Ezinne and Abigail drill and review each other's work every week.
