## A letter from Debo

Dorathy,

This is the longest document anybody at this firm has been handed, and I want to explain why you are the one getting it.

Consult for Africa has a demand problem that does not look like a demand problem. We are carrying more engagements than we have people, almost every one of them larger now than when it began, and the book has grown every month this year. From the inside that reads as success. What it actually is, is a firm living entirely off one person's relationships, which is me, and that is not a growth engine, it is a bottleneck with good manners. Every engagement in Part Three arrived because somebody I worked with at Cedarcrest or Evercare or through Doctoora picked up the phone. None of it arrived because the firm went and found it.

Alongside the consulting we have built products, and the reason matters. The same three things break in every African health institution we are called into, which are leadership, workforce and the economics of a practice, and I got tired of recommending fixes that nobody in this market could actually buy. So we built them. Maarova for leadership, CadreHealth for workforce, Mezo for the practice. They work. They are built, they are deployed, they hold real data and real users, and between them they have produced almost no revenue, because building a product and selling one are different jobs and we have only ever done the first.

That is the job. You own the growth of the firm's own revenue and you own the growth of the products, and those are not two jobs loosely related, they are the same job seen from two ends, because the products are what let us sell to a hospital that cannot afford a consulting engagement and the consulting is what gets the products into a building where somebody will use them.

A few things I want to say plainly.

I am not asking you to run a sales team, because there is not one, and the first thing you will probably tell me is that there needs to be. What I am asking for is that the pipeline stops being a function of my diary. Read Part Four before you read anything else, because it is the shortest part of this document and the most uncomfortable: our pipeline on the platform holds one lead, which has been sitting untouched since the eighteenth of September, and four proposals that have been in draft since March. That is not a tooling problem. It is what happens when nobody owns the pipe.

On the products, you are going to find that the problem is almost never the product. It is that nobody was told. Part Five sets out the measurement that makes this unarguable: on Mezo, conversion is about sixty eight per cent among the doctors who were actually told they had a place, and zero out of nine hundred and seventy among those who were not. Every product in the firm has a version of that number, and the lever is the same lever each time.

I will give you the relationships. Ask me how I know somebody before you write to them, every single time, because I usually do know them and a correct but cold letter to a friend of fifteen years costs more than a late one. That is not a formality and it is the one rule I will be irritable about.

The last thing is confidentiality, which I am strict about. Everything here is real, including the client names, the findings and the shape of the deals, because you cannot sell what you do not understand. None of it leaves the firm. We describe our work by method and lesson, never by client, and in a market this small a combination of harmless details identifies somebody.

On timing. You are full time from January, so this pack is deliberately arriving three months early, and that is the best thing about it. Nobody gets to read their firm properly before they start. You do. Read Parts Four and Five first, then Three, then the rest, and do it across the next few weeks rather than the next few days.

What I do not want is for the January start to read as three quiet months. Two things are worth doing now. Tell me where you think I am wrong, because a view formed before you are inside the machine is worth more than one formed after. And keep the Aman engagement moving, because that is already yours and it is the only part of this book you have actually run.

The things in this pack with dates on them are mine until January, not yours. Part Eight says which ones and who is holding each, because a handover list that assumes you are already here would have you worrying in October about work you cannot touch until the new year.

The natural first step is for you and me to sit down and settle three things: what we do about the pipeline between now and January, whether the products get a campaign or a parking space, and your own terms, which Part Two is honest about being unsettled.

Warm regards,

Dr Debo Odulana
Founding Partner, Consult for Africa

### 1.2 The argument the firm rests on

Across the continent the gap between a struggling hospital and a thriving one is rarely the building or the equipment. It is execution, leadership and people. That is not a slogan reverse engineered for a website, it is what every engagement has found independently, which is why a paediatric centre that looked like it had a stock control problem turned out to have an incentives problem, and a surgeon who believed his growth was limited by his clinic's postcode turned out to be limited by a referral pipeline he had never built.

Debo's blunter version is worth remembering, because it is also a sales argument: the presenting cause is always a thing, and the reason is never the thing. A client who tells you their problem is the equipment is telling you what they have already tried to fix.

### 1.5 How demand actually arrives, and what that means for you

Almost entirely by referral and through Debo's own network. That is the single most important fact about this firm's commercial position, and it cuts two ways.

It is why the win rate is high and the sales cycle is short, because a prospect who arrives through a mutual friend is already most of the way sold. It is also why the pipeline is thin, lumpy and unforecastable, because nobody can schedule a referral.

The progression that recurs has five stages and it is worth knowing by name, because each stage has a different deliverable and a different conversation.

**The options note** comes first, before a prospect has engaged us at all. It is a short note laying out the strategic options, giving capital and revenue as ranges rather than point estimates, stating what has to be true and what kills each option, and being explicit about what we cannot yet know. It is deliberately not a feasibility study, because a confident twenty six page plan answers every question and leaves nobody a reason to call. This is your most important document and you will write a lot of them.

**The diagnostic** is where we go in and find out what is actually there, which is where our methods live and where almost all our best proof comes from.

**The strategy or the plan** is built on the diagnostic and presented as decisions with options rather than recommendations, so the board is choosing rather than being told.

**Delivery** is execution with a named team and a milestone schedule.

**Operate** is where we run it under a management agreement with an operator fee, and it is where the annuity is.

Not every engagement goes the whole way and several have. The commercial implication is that our case studies are rarely "we advised and left", and that is our single biggest differentiator against both the international firms and the local boutiques. It is also why the operate stage matters more than its share of today's revenue suggests: a management agreement is recurring, it compounds, and it is very hard for a client to unwind.

### 1.6 How we price, and where the rate cards live

The numbers are not in this document. The logic is, and the logic is what you will actually argue with.

**Resourced days.** Delivery work is priced as named people for a number of days at published grades, plus a programme rate on top, which is currently ten per cent. This is the base for audits, strategy and setup work. It matters because it means a discount conversation is a conversation about scope or seniority, not about a mysterious number.

**Standard, then one visible concession, then net.** Our proposals show the full standard fee, a single named concession line, and the net. Full transparency about what has been given up. This is deliberate, it works, and it is why we never show a pre discounted price as though it were the price.

**Anchor, do not open at the floor.** We publish an opening position and hold concessions back for the negotiation. We never volunteer a cap, never benchmark ourselves at the bottom of a comparator range in a client document, and never defend a price before anybody has questioned it. Several files have a private negotiation guide in the repository carrying the floor and the concession ladder, and those are never sent to a client under any circumstances.

**Build and operate has five separate bases**, because it is five different activities: delivery, capital efficiency, establishment, management and equity. One finding from that work is worth carrying into any venture conversation: a percentage of capital development fee is the wrong instrument whenever the client wants lean capital, because it pays us for spending their money. We replaced it with a fixed delivery fee plus a share of the capital saved.

**Performance and success fees are where Debo is taking the firm.** House of Refuge runs a fundraising mandate charged as a share of funds raised. Dennis Ashley is priced as a share of collections. Arabella's forward path is a share of monthly revenue with a floor in the early months. The Medbury division carries a performance share payable only when the board's revenue goal and its margin goal are both met. If you are shaping a new deal, that is the direction of travel and Debo will back you for proposing it.

**Where the figures are.** On the platform, at **/finance/rate-card**, which exists for this role. It carries the published day rates by grade, the programme rate, the recruitment position and the one it replaced, the leadership assessment and coaching menu with the coach economics, the member side subscription, the five bases of a build and operate mandate shown as anchor against walk away, the negotiation doctrine, the terms never to concede, and the path to every private concession ladder the firm holds. It also carries the two calibration points that tell you where the floor really is, which matter more than the card itself.

**It opens for you in January, with the rest of your access, and there is no need to chase it before then.** The page is gated to the senior roles rather than to Finance, because the office reads invoice status in order to chase and sees no rates at all, and that grant goes with the start rather than ahead of it. Nothing you can usefully do between now and January requires a number off it.

Two things are not on it, on purpose. Client balances and agreed fees, because those live on the engagement record and under Invoices, which is the only place they are ever current, so read a client number there on the day you quote it. And agency pricing, which does not exist yet, covered in Part Five.

One rule that applies to you more than to anybody else in the firm: **never put a price in marketing copy and never defend a price nobody has challenged.** If a piece of content provokes a fee conversation, that is the correct outcome, and the conversation happens on a call.

## Part Two: Your role, and what is unsettled about it

### 2.1 What the role holds

The title in the firm's own documents is **Director of Business Development and Growth**, and the handbook describes you as owning the business development practice and the payer and corporate relationships. In practice the role has four pieces and they are not equally built.

**The firm's pipeline.** Leads, discovery calls, proposals, qualification, and the follow through on all of it. This exists and is in poor condition, which Part Four covers.

**The products' growth.** Maarova, CadreHealth, Mezo, and the channels that were built to distribute them and never used. This is the largest piece of genuinely new ground and it is where the most value is sitting unclaimed.

**Payer and corporate relationships.** Health insurers, corporate accounts, employers. You are already the practice lead on the Aman engagement, which is a payer network build, so this is the piece you have actually done here.

**The commercial shape of new deals.** Not setting price, which is Debo's, but proposing structure, because a growth role that cannot shape a deal is a lead generation function with a better title.

### 2.2 What the platform says about you, and what changes in January

You have held a platform account since the fifteenth of May 2026 under your own email address. Your profile records you in Abuja, ten years of experience, tier experienced, primary specialty business development, specialty category strategy, with expertise logged in health insurance, revenue cycle, marketing, content and training. Your bio describes healthcare business development, health insurer partnerships and revenue cycle management, with a track record on corporate partnerships and commercial transformation across hospital networks. That is all correct and it is a good record.

Three things about it are out of date, and none of them is urgent, which is why they are listed here rather than in the January checklist.

**Your platform role is CONSULTANT and your recorded title is Business Development Manager.** You are Director of Business Development and Growth, which is how the marketing handbook and the Aman strategy both name you, so the record is simply behind. It is worth knowing rather than fixing today, because role is what gates access here: the consultant role reaches the consultant surfaces and not the commercial ones, so the pipeline and the rate card are not open to you yet. That changes when you start, as one act rather than as a series of requests, and it is deliberately tied to the start date rather than run ahead of it.

What it means in the meantime is practical. If a page tells you that you do not have access, it is the role rather than a fault, so do not spend time on it. The Aman engagement and your consultant surfaces work as they always have.

**No rates are set on your profile and no CV is on file.** Both fields are empty, which matters once you are resourced onto a client engagement as a named person on a resourced day schedule, because the schedule prices off the grade. Worth sending Debo a CV before January so it is not being chased in your first week.

### 2.3 Who owns what, so you are not negotiating boundaries in month three

This is the part most likely to cause friction, so it is written down.

**You and Nimi.** Marketing's job in a referral led business is not to generate cold leads, it is to make the firm findable, give a referrer something to send, shorten the time from first conversation to signature by having the proof already published, and build the standing that makes an owner take the call. So Nimi builds the proof and you use it. The highest value thing marketing can do for you is clear named references, and right now exactly one is cleared, which is that the firm is the management partner for a twenty one bed drug and addiction rehabilitation centre in Lekki. There is a library of ten anonymised case studies written up and not one has been published. Getting two or three more names cleared is a joint piece of work and the moment to ask a client is when an engagement is going well, not months later.

**You and Oludayo.** You sell workforce and recruitment mandates, she delivers them. The pricing position on recruitment is hers to hold and Debo's to set, and it moved in September, so read section 5.5 before you quote anybody.

**You and Tito.** She runs delivery on audits. Before you promise a timeline on a diagnostic, it goes past her, because the firm's capacity is the real constraint on how much you can sell and it is not visible from the commercial side.

**You and Ezinne.** She runs the Thursday pipeline sweep, which chases every lead untouched for fourteen days and every proposal older than a week. She owns the hygiene and the chasing. You own the deal. That division only works if the pipeline record is current, which means the discipline of logging a conversation is not administrative tidiness, it is what makes somebody else able to chase for you.

**You and Debo.** He owns price and he owns the relationships. You own structure, qualification and follow through. Where the two meet is the concession ladder, and the rule there is that you never volunteer the next concession.

### 2.4 How Debo works

He is fast, he wants things done rather than discussed, and he does not want trailing summaries. A short, specific update beats a complete one. If a thing is decided, say what was decided; if it is stuck, say what you need from him in one line.

He reads on his phone between meetings, often late. Anything that needs a decision should be answerable with a yes, a no or a number, and if it genuinely cannot be, it belongs on the decisions register with the options and your recommendation written out rather than in a message.

**He often knows the person you are writing to**, frequently from Cedarcrest, Evercare or Doctoora. Always ask how he knows somebody before you draft anything that goes to them. This is the rule he will be most irritable about, because a correct but cold letter to a friend of fifteen years costs more than a late one, and in this market there is no recovering from it.

### 2.5 What is unsettled, and should be settled early

Your own terms are not recorded anywhere the platform can see, which is the same gap as the missing rates on your profile. Whether you are staff from January, a director on a practice arrangement, or resourced per engagement changes how you are paid and what you can commit the firm to. Settle it before the start rather than discovering it at the first invoice run, because a January start with unsettled terms turns the first fortnight into an administrative conversation instead of a commercial one.

What you are doing between now and then is also worth agreeing explicitly rather than leaving to drift, because the two obvious answers are different sizes. Reading this pack and forming a view is one thing. Carrying the Aman engagement, which is already yours, is another. Picking up anything from Part Eight's held list is a third and it would need a separate arrangement.

Separately, there is no business development budget, no sales team, no customer relationship system beyond the platform's own Communications surface, and no agency rate card for the service line you would be selling into the Grandville conversation. None of those is a reason not to start. All of them are things to put in front of Debo as decisions with options rather than as complaints, and the three months before you start are a better time to do that than the three weeks after.

### 5.4 Maarova

**What it is.** A psychometric assessment and coaching platform for healthcare leaders, calibrated to the African context rather than translated into it. The tagline is "assess, develop, and retain Africa's healthcare leaders." The buyer is almost never the person being assessed, which is the most important commercial fact about it: you are selling to hospital groups, training hospitals, health system foundations, boards and HR directors who are hiring or developing frontline, middle and executive leaders.

**The three streams**, and every Maarova organisation is set up on one, which decides which modules run. Recruitment is assessment as part of a hiring decision, sold as "hire with science, not gut feel." Development is assessment as the opening of a coaching relationship, sold as "assess, coach, measure, repeat." Intelligence is assessment across a whole leadership population to see the landscape, sold as "see your entire leadership landscape."

**The six modules.** Behavioural style, values, emotional intelligence, clinical leadership transition, culture, and a 360 feedback module. The clinical leadership module is deliberately restricted to clinical professionals so a non clinical leader is not penalised for not having it, and the 360 is decoupled from the core because it depends on external raters and takes days or weeks. Results appear progressively as each module completes, so a participant gets value immediately rather than at the end.

**The methodology, which is the strongest and most underused sales asset in the firm.** This is what distinguishes Maarova from every off the shelf instrument in this market, and almost none of it has ever been published.

The Hofstede scores that most consultancies still quote for Nigeria come from employee surveys conducted between 1967 and 1973, and the Nigeria figure was derived from a three country West African aggregate rather than a Nigeria specific sample, so Maarova treats Hofstede as background literature rather than as a load bearing framework. The integrity backbone is HEXACO, whose honesty and humility factor outperforms every Big Five dimension as a predictor of counterproductive work behaviour and which has been validated in Nigerian samples. That is combined with the South African Personality Inventory, the most rigorous indigenously developed African personality assessment in existence, built across all eleven official South African languages from how native speakers describe personality rather than by translating a Western inventory, and whose integrity factor weights fairness and resistance to discrimination, which fits the African pattern of integrity failure better than a greed weighted Western construct does. Response bias is handled through the practice versus values gap from the GLOBE study, which measured each dimension twice, once as what people do and once as what people think should happen, because the size of that gap predicts which items will pull answers toward the aspirational pole. The response format direction is quasi ipsative forced choice scored with Thurstonian item response theory, which is materially harder to fake than a rating scale and supports valid comparison between candidates.

The coaching differentiator is the **Ubuntu framework**, where goals are framed around collective impact rather than individual achievement and the coaching question is how your leadership serves your community, with the coach positioned as a thought partner rather than an authority so that power distance is respected rather than ignored. No validated Ubuntu leadership instrument exists anywhere, which is an opportunity and it is ours to take.

There is coaching evidence worth quoting in a room. The single strongest predictor of coaching outcomes is the working alliance, which is the quality of the relationship, at a correlation of roughly point four one, and that is more predictive than credentials, session count or methodology. Mayo Clinic found six coaching sessions significantly reduced physician emotional exhaustion. Industry return on investment is reported at five to eight times when measured on productivity and retention.

**The coaching product shape.** One to one is twelve sessions over six months, biweekly, by video plus messaging. Group coaching is four to six peer leaders meeting monthly over three to six months. On site workshops run half or full day for ten to thirty leaders. Leadership retreats run two to three days offsite for ten to twenty. An annual conference for fifty to two hundred is the thought leadership vehicle. Coaches are sourced from the certified coaching bodies across Nigeria, South Africa and Kenya, with a target acceptance rate of ten to fifteen per cent as a quality signal, and a fifteen minute chemistry call before any engagement starts, which is non negotiable.

**The pricing argument, without the numbers.** The anchors are Lagos Business School's Advanced Management Programme and its Chief Executive Programme, and Harvard's global health leaders programme. Against those, an enterprise cohort of ten leaders costs a hospital about the same as sending one person to Lagos Business School, and an assessment on its own costs less than a conference registration. That comparison is the sales argument and it is a strong one. The rate card itself is in the pricing strategy note and carries a markup applied in May to absorb the Founding Circle discount, with coaches receiving sixty five to seventy per cent of the coaching component.

## Part Seven: How we sell, and the rules

### 7.1 The methods we reuse, which are also the pitch

These have names inside the firm and each one is both a delivery method and a reason to be hired. Knowing them lets you describe how we work in a room without promising a deliverable.

**The four stream diagnostic** and **the ten case trace**, where ten real cases are picked on the morning of a visit and each followed through a twenty two link chain from first enquiry to current outcome, so that every gap found is specific rather than theoretical.

**The forced tradeoff leadership survey**, where respondents allocate a fixed number of points rather than rating everything highly, which is how you get a real answer out of a leadership team.

**Measured geometry**, which is the habit of recovering the facts from source rather than accepting the brief. It found that the Lyfe Place campus was substantially larger than the brief said and that the extra area was all corridor, which changed the entire financial case.

**Decisions with options, not recommendations**, so a board chooses rather than being told.

**The options note**, which is the stage one document described in section 1.5.

**Standard, concession, net**, which is how every fee is presented.

**Give to get**, which is how both the salary map and the hospital reviews are unlocked, and which is a genuine design principle rather than a growth hack.

**One link, not six attachments.** A client gets a single unindexed page carrying everything they need. Two are live, for Arabella and for Osteon. This is a surprisingly strong differentiator in a market where everybody else sends a zip file.

**Verify the destination before you send**, because a live page can sit in front of a dead endpoint and a success code can sit in front of a parser that read nothing.

**Check every claim of absence.** When somebody tells you a thing does not exist, verify it, because in this firm's experience the thing usually exists and nobody looked.

### 7.2 The proof ladder, and why you are short of proof

Published proof is what a referral led business runs on, because the person who was told about us will look us up, and the person doing the telling needs something to send.

What exists is a library of ten anonymised case studies, written up and cleared for use in anonymised form, covering a facility whose revenue record turned out to be a nursing handover sheet, a surgeon who blamed his postcode, a building two hundred square metres bigger than its brief, a chief executive who would not override her own managers, a board of eight split four to four, a phase three decision that had to be taken in phase one, counting providers when the number that mattered was coverage, a venture that had its sequence backwards, an audit of our own instrument, and a policy brief on the housemanship bottleneck. **Not one of them has been published.** Three insights articles drawn from them would change how the firm reads to anybody who looks us up.

What does not exist is named references, where there is exactly one cleared. Getting two or three more is the highest value business development task available and the moment to ask a client is when an engagement is going well, not months later.

### 7.3 Confidentiality and the anonymisation standard

When our work is described publicly, a client is identified by sector, setting, scale and stage, never by name, owner, district, or any combination of details that would identify them in a market this small. Ratios may be published and absolute figures tied to an identifiable client may not.

Two files are excluded from external use absolutely, which are Haven's clinical incident material and Havana's family politics. The Havana board situation in particular is not to be discussed outside the firm at all.

### 7.4 Writing to senior clinicians

There is a separate set of rules for outreach to consultants, emeritus physicians, retired clinicians and the diaspora, and it is worth reading before your first campaign rather than after it. The principle underneath all of them is that these are people who have been pitched by everybody and who read application language as junior.

That is why Mezo is a private practice opportunity rather than an account to claim, why CadreHealth talks to the cadre rather than to the system, and why migration is framed as a choice the professional is making rather than as an exit we are facilitating. Match the copy to the receipts: we earn trust with real salary numbers and real reviews, so aspirational copy without the data behind it is worse than nothing with this audience.

### 7.5 How we write, and the things that read as generated

Debo rejected a draft in his own name in September with the note that it read as machine written, and the content had been right. The problem was the furniture. Avoid all six of these: three paragraphs opening with matched constructions, the short pivot sentence where a long line is followed by a four word correction, an epigram at the end of every paragraph because one aphorism is a voice and four is a machine, a line commenting on the document itself, paragraphs all the same length, and enumerating in prose with first, second and third.

What works instead is opening with what was actually done rather than a framing sentence, varying paragraph length hard, letting a blunt opinion sit without a line explaining it, and cutting about a third of the words. His own rhythm is long connected sentences joined with because, so and which, explaining as they go.

In a client document the client is you and the firm is we. First person singular is reserved for Debo's own letters. Name the client's people by role and warmly, so that a finding about a system never reads as a finding about a person. We do not perform enthusiasm, we do not use exclamation marks, and we say what we cannot tell them, because candour about the limits of evidence reads as integrity and it protects the firm.

**Two hard rules.** Never use an em dash, in a document, a headline, a message or interface text; use a comma, a full stop, a colon, or restructure the sentence. And never name our platforms in client body copy.

The sender of record is hello@consultforafrica.com, and that matters because it is the mailbox Debo checks, so anything sent as something else leaves no evidence where he will look for it. Bulk and outreach sending goes through the transactional mail provider's interface only, because the ordinary office mail system throttles batches and starts refusing them partway through, and a half sent campaign is worse than an unsent one. Client documents are built as PDFs from scripts in the repository and land in the documents folder, which is how a figure in a summary cannot disagree with the figure in the full document. They are not web pages and they are not pasted into email bodies.

For a proposal or a pitch to a principal, the deliverable is a landscape deck and the prose document is the leave behind. Reports, letters and memoranda stay as prose. ## Appendix D: The standing checklists

### Before you write to anybody outside the firm

- [ ] Ask Debo how he knows them
- [ ] Check the client or lead record for what we have already sent them
- [ ] Check whether they hold a platform, portal or product account already
- [ ] No platform names in the body copy
- [ ] No em dash
- [ ] No price unless Debo has cleared the number
- [ ] Sender is hello@, and the send is logged in Communications with a next action

### Before you quote a price

- [ ] Read the figure off the current source, not from a document or from memory
- [ ] Confirm the standard, the single named concession and the net
- [ ] Confirm what is excluded, particularly tax and government or regulatory fees
- [ ] Confirm the payment shape: what is due when, and what triggers each part
- [ ] Terms block present: non refundable, free substitution, one reschedule
- [ ] Nothing from a private negotiation guide has leaked into the client document

### Qualifying an opportunity

- [ ] Who actually decides, and have we met them
- [ ] What happens if they do nothing, because that is the real competitor
- [ ] Is there money, and is it in this budget year
- [ ] Can we deliver it, confirmed with Tito or Oludayo rather than assumed
- [ ] What is the stage two of this, because the first sale is rarely the business
- [ ] Stage, next action and date on the pipeline record, and a qualification note written

### Before any bulk send to a product audience

- [ ] Suppression list current, and the historical back fill done
- [ ] Salutation built with the helper, not with a last name field
- [ ] One real message sent to a real destination and the record checked
- [ ] Sending through the transactional provider, never the office mail system
- [ ] Senior medic register, and the aspirational framing where the audience is consultants
- [ ] Debo has seen the copy
