## Part Two: Your role, and how this office runs

### 2.1 What the role holds

The role is the diary, the inbox, travel, meeting preparation and minutes, and the follow up chasing across everything the firm has in flight. Alongside that you carry client work, because everybody at CFA works, and that is why your platform access reaches the delivery surfaces and not only the office ones.

Four things are yours specifically rather than shared with the Administrative Assistant, and they are the ones that make the role senior. You can press send on a communication or a campaign, you can read finance so you can chase money, you hold organiser rights on meetings so you can reschedule, cancel and control the recording bot, and you assign and review work.

### 2.2 The delegation chain

Work flows from Debo to you to Abigail, and review flows back the same way. He reviews you, you review her, and if he finds himself reviewing her work directly that is treated as a fault in the chain rather than a shortcut, because it undercuts you and puts him back in the loop the office exists to get him out of.

This is built into the platform rather than left to good intentions. The person who assigns a task is the person who reviews it, so when Abigail marks something blocked it raises to you. You can break one of Debo's tasks into sub tasks for her, and he sees the roll up on his original without being in the detail.

### 2.3 The rules of the task system

These are not suggestions, they are the standard the office is judged on.

A task goes out with a **brief** explaining why it matters and what it feeds into, and a **definition of done** describing what finished looks like in the assignee's words rather than yours. Neither is written verbally and neither is skipped because the task seems obvious.

Anything new to the assignee gets a **worked example** attached the first time. The reason is on the record. The first office inventory count came back in ten minutes against a three hour estimate with nothing actually recorded, because a definition of done had described a thing the person had never seen.

A **check in date** is set at the moment of assignment and is not renegotiated later, so a five day task gets a day two conversation instead of a day five surprise.

The assigner puts an **estimate** on the task and the assignee can see it. If the real time comes in at triple, the estimate was wrong and both people learn something.

Two statuses carry most of the weight. **Blocked** lets somebody stop without interrupting anyone and without it reading as failure. **Changes requested** sends work back with a written note rather than having it silently redone, which is the habit that actually kills delegation, because an unwritten correction has to be made again next month.

The measure of whether this office is working is not how much gets done in month one. It is whether the volume of work going out with written briefs rises while the volume coming back for changes falls.

### 2.4 What you can reach, and what is deliberately withheld

Your access was granted surface by surface rather than inherited from a role list, which is why some things you might expect are absent on purpose.

**Yours:** tasks, campaigns, commitments, decisions, the pipeline, communications, meetings, the operating rhythm, invoices and payment status read only, knowledge, the asset library and the office inventory.

**Withheld for now, to be revisited at the three month mark:** finance write access, consultant rates, agent commissions, the admin area including users and assessments, Maarova coaching records and Founding Circle applications because those are personal development records, client contracts and non disclosure agreements, and the partner, client, agent and consultant portals.

Nothing in that second list is a comment on you. It is how the office was designed, so that access is always granted deliberately and the places where your role and Abigail's differ stay visible.

### 2.5 The operating rhythm

Five standing items already exist on the platform, and they generate real tasks on a cadence so that a missed month shows up as a gap rather than as silence. Four currently sit with Abigail and you review them. The fifth has been waiting for you.

### 2.6 How Debo works

He is fast, he wants things done rather than discussed, and he does not want trailing summaries. A short, specific update beats a complete one. If a thing is decided, say what was decided; if it is stuck, say what you need from him in one line.

He reads on his phone between meetings, often late. Anything that needs a decision should be able to be answered with a yes, a no or a number, and if it genuinely cannot, it belongs on the decisions register with the options and your recommendation written out rather than in a message.

He often knows the person you are writing to, frequently from Cedarcrest, Evercare or Doctoora. **Always ask how he knows somebody before you draft anything that goes to them**, because a correct but cold letter to a friend of fifteen years costs more than a late one.

### 2.7 The three month mark

At three months the withheld list gets revisited, which in practice means finance write access and some of the admin surfaces. What earns that is not tenure. It is the review backlog staying empty, the chase register being current, and Abigail's work arriving finished because the briefs she is given are good.

## Part Seven: How we write, and what never leaves the building

### 7.1 The register

We write to people rather than about them. Our clients are senior, most are clinicians, and several know Debo personally, which sets the register for everything.

In a client document the client is **you** and the firm is **we**. First person singular is reserved for Debo's own letters and for the judgements he is personally making. Name the client's people by role and warmly, so a finding about a system never reads as a finding about a person.

We do not perform enthusiasm, we do not use exclamation marks, and we do not write a sentence that a consultant reading it on a phone between cases would find embarrassing.

Say what we cannot tell them. Candour about the limits of evidence reads as integrity, and it protects the firm.

### 7.2 The things that make writing read as generated

Debo rejected a draft in his own name in September with the note that it read as machine written, and the content had been right. The problem was the furniture. Avoid all six of these.

- Three paragraphs opening with matched constructions
- The short pivot sentence, where a long line is followed by a four word correction
- An epigram at the end of every paragraph, because one aphorism is a voice and four is a machine
- A line commenting on the document itself
- Paragraphs all the same length, and justified text
- Enumerating in prose with first, second and third

What works instead is opening with what was actually done rather than a framing sentence, varying paragraph length hard, letting a blunt opinion sit without a line explaining it, and cutting about a third of the words.

His own rhythm is long connected sentences joined with because, so and which, explaining as they go. Staccato declaratives and constructions of the form not this but that are the tell, even when every word is right.

### 7.3 Two hard rules on characters and names

**Never use an em dash.** Not in a document, not in a headline, not in a message, not in interface text. Use a comma, a full stop, a colon, or restructure the sentence.

**Never name our platforms in client body copy.** Describe what is done. The only exception is an appendix the client has specifically asked for.

### 7.4 Confidentiality and the anonymisation standard

Everything in this pack is internal. When our work is described publicly, a client is identified by sector, setting, scale and stage, never by name, owner, district, or any combination of details that would identify them in a market this small. Ratios may be published and absolute naira figures tied to an identifiable client may not.

Two files are excluded from external use absolutely, which are Haven's clinical incident material and Havana's family politics.

### 7.5 Before you write to anybody outside the firm

Ask Debo how he knows them. He usually does, and the cold register insults an old relationship.

For outreach to senior clinicians, consultants, emeritus physicians and the diaspora, there is a separate set of rules about what to say and what never to say, and it is worth reading before your first campaign rather than after it.

### 7.6 Email, and the two mistakes that have been made before

The sender of record is **hello@consultforafrica.com**, and that matters because it is the mailbox Debo checks, so anything sent as something else leaves no evidence where he will look for it.

Bulk and outreach sending goes through the transactional mail provider's interface only. The ordinary office mail system throttles batches and starts refusing them partway through, which has happened, and a half sent campaign is worse than an unsent one.

And before any bulk send, send one real message to a real destination and check that the record actually arrived, because a live page can sit in front of a dead endpoint and a success code can sit in front of a parser that read nothing.

### 7.7 Deliverables

Client documents are built as PDFs from scripts in the repository and land in the documents folder, which is how a figure in a summary cannot disagree with the figure in the full document. They are not web pages and they are not pasted into email bodies.

For a proposal or a pitch to a principal, the deliverable is a landscape deck and the prose document is the leave behind. Reports, letters and memoranda stay as prose.

## Appendix B: Glossary

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Audit, organisational | Our structured diagnostic across ten domains of a facility, ending in a report and a set of board decisions |
| Build and operate | We design and set up a facility or venture, then run it under a management agreement |
| CAC | Corporate Affairs Commission. Incorporation and company filings |
| CSSD | Central sterile services department. Where instruments are sterilised, and a catastrophic risk point in surgery |
| Day case | A procedure where the patient goes home the same day |
| DoA | Delegation of authority. The written list of what a board keeps and what it delegates |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. The operating profit measure in every one of our models |
| EMR | Electronic medical record |
| FCDA and AMMC | Federal Capital Development Authority and the area municipal council. Development control, including change of use |
| Fractional leadership | A senior operator placed part time into a client, such as an interim chief operating officer |
| HEFAMAA | The Lagos State facility monitoring and accreditation agency. Abuja has its own route |
| HMO | Health maintenance organisation. A health insurer, and the payer behind much of a hospital's revenue |
| MDCN | Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria. Registers doctors and accredits internship places |
| Medipark | A campus of several independent clinical businesses sharing a building and its services |
| MOU | Memorandum of understanding |
| NAFDAC | The regulator for medicines, devices and implants |
| NDPA 2023 | Nigeria Data Protection Act, which replaced the 2019 regulation. Say NDPA, not NDPR |
| NICU | Neonatal intensive care unit. Three cots at Haven, and the highest value admission in the building |
| NNRA | Nigerian Nuclear Regulatory Authority. Radiation sources, imaging and radiotherapy |
| Panel, insurance | The list of facilities an insurer will pay. Being on it is the commercial gate |
| RC number | A company's registration number at the Corporate Affairs Commission |
| Resourced days | How we price delivery work, as named people for a number of days at published rates, plus a programme rate |
| Secondment | Someone employed by one party and directed by another. The structure of the two Medbury operating officers |
| Sessional | A doctor who books a session in a clinic rather than being employed by it |
| SPV | Special purpose vehicle. A company formed for one venture |
| Takaful | Islamic cooperative insurance. The structure Aman is built on |
| Tariff | A facility's price list, line by line |
| VMI | Vendor managed inventory. The supplier holds the stock and is paid on use, which frees working capital |
| WHT | Withholding tax. We deduct 5 per cent from contractor payments and remit it |

## Appendix C: The standing checklists

### The Monday partner meeting pack, prepared by Friday evening

- [ ] Every active engagement, one line each, with status
- [ ] Anything that slipped since last week, and why
- [ ] Decisions needed from the partners, with options and a recommendation
- [ ] The diary for the week ahead

- [ ] Every lead with no contact in fourteen days chased, or a written reason on it
- [ ] Every proposal sent more than a week ago followed up
- [ ] Anything needing a partner rather than you on the commitment register with a name against it
- [ ] Draft proposals that were never sent either sent or closed

- [ ] Every engagement with billable work has an invoice raised and approved
- [ ] Terms block present: non refundable, free substitution, one reschedule
- [ ] Tax shown separately, correct entity name, correct bank details
- [ ] Anything that cannot be invoiced has a written reason
- [ ] The list of what went out is with Debo

### The overdue chase, 15th of the month

- [ ] Every invoice past due chased this month
- [ ] A promised payment date recorded against each one
- [ ] Disputes escalated rather than chased again

- [ ] Decisions, each with a name and a date
- [ ] Commitments, split by whether we owe it or they do, onto the register
- [ ] Anything that became a task, assigned with a brief and a definition of done
- [ ] Out the same day, because a minute that arrives on Thursday is a record and one that arrives within the hour is a tool
