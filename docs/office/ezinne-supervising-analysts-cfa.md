## 3. Briefing and reviewing

### 3.1 Writing a task

Every task needs four things before you assign it.

- Brief: why the work matters and what it feeds into, in two or three sentences.
- Definition of done: what finished looks like, specific enough that they can check it themselves.
- Worked example: the first time they do any type of task, attach an example of one that is already right. A past set of minutes, a past sweep, a past summary.
- Dates: a due date and a check-in date roughly halfway. A five-day task gets a day-two conversation.

Weak definition of done: "Update the Haven record."
Strong definition of done: "Haven notes show every meeting since 1 October with date, attendees and actions; every action has an owner and a due date; documents are filed against their milestone."

### 3.2 Reviewing

- Approve when it meets the definition of done, even if you would have done it differently.
- Request changes in writing on the task, never only verbally. Say what is wrong and what right looks like. The note is what they learn from.
- Two rounds of changes on the same thing usually means the brief was unclear. Rewrite the brief, not the person.
- Record what you see. The evaluation is written from the task history, so the task history has to be true.

### 3.3 When they are blocked

- Answer within one working day, even if the answer is "I will find out by Thursday."
- If the block is a client not replying, it is not theirs to fix. Put it on the commitment register with the engagement lead's name.
- A block raised early is good work. Say so when it happens, so they keep doing it.

### 3.4 When two people give them work

The engagement leads will also ask them for things. You set priority. If an analyst tells you two requests clash, decide within the day and tell both requesters.

## 4. The weekly check-in

Twenty minutes, one at a time, same slot each week. Four questions in this order.

1. What did you finish this week?
2. What is stuck, and what would unstick it?
3. What is coming next week that worries you?
4. What do you want to get better at?

Write two or three lines on each analyst after the check-in, in a note only you keep. Those notes are what you evaluate from on the 28th.

---

## 5. The monthly evaluation

### 5.1 Steps

1. By the 25th: read the month's task history for each analyst (submitted, changes requested, blocked, overdue) and your check-in notes.
2. By the 26th: ask each for three lines (what they delivered, what went wrong, what they want to learn).
3. On or before the 28th: a 30-minute conversation with each. Tell them the scores and why, and agree one development goal for next month.
4. Same day: record the evaluation under Admin, then Intern Programme. Open their rotation and choose "Record this month's evaluation". The period fills itself in (Month 1, Month 2 and so on).
5. Mark the monthly evaluation task done on your desk.

The conversation comes before the form. Nothing on the form should be news to them.

### 5.2 How to score

Five areas, each 1 to 5. The platform averages them.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Not yet doing the job; most work comes back or is late without warning |
| 2 | Doing parts of it; needs close direction on routine work |
| 3 | Doing the job well; routine work is right the first time. This is the expected score. |
| 4 | Doing the job well and more; spots and fixes problems unprompted |
| 5 | Operating at the level above; rare, and you should be able to name the evidence |

What to look at in each area:

| Area | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| Technical skills | Accuracy of engagement records and minutes; how many submissions needed changes; quality of first drafts |
| Communication | Written updates clear without a follow-up question; blocks raised early with a reason; minutes a client could read |
| Professionalism | Due dates met, or renegotiated before they passed; confidentiality; time recorded honestly |
| Initiative | Gaps they found and fixed without being asked; questions that improved the work |
| Teamwork | Help given to each other and to Abigail; clean handovers; how they take a change request |

Rules for scoring:

- Score the month, not the person. A good month after a bad one is a good month.
- Start everyone at 3 and move only on evidence you can point to.
- Do not compare Favour and Ruth to each other in the form or the conversation. Compare each to the definition of the job.
- Month 1 will usually sit around 2 to 3. That is normal for someone new, and you should tell them so.

### 5.3 Writing the feedback

- Strengths: at least one, specific. "The Osteon minutes on 12 November were up the same afternoon with every action owned" is useful. "Hardworking" is not.
- Areas to develop: at least one, specific, and something they can act on next month.
- Comments: the agreed development goal for next month, in one line.

### 5.4 Promotion

From month three, tick "recommend promotion" only when an analyst has scored 4 or above in at least three areas for two months running. The tick goes to Debo as a recommendation, not a decision.

## 6. When something is not working

| Situation | What you do |
|---|---|
| Work is repeatedly late | Ask in the check-in what is getting in the way. Usually it is too many requesters or an unclear brief. Fix that first. |
| Quality is not improving after two months | Write down the specific pattern, agree one change with them, and review it weekly for a month. Tell Debo only if it has not moved by then. |
| A confidentiality breach | Stop and tell Debo the same day. Do not handle it alone. |
| They are unhappy or overwhelmed | Lighten the load for a week and say why. It is cheaper than losing them. |

Keep any concern about an individual between you, them and Debo. Do not raise it in front of Abigail or the other analyst.
