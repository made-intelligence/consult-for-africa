## How to mark

- Trainees: Ezinne Orji and Abigail Oladejo.
- Weekly test: 22 questions, one mark each. Question 20 is five dictated spellings: one mark if all five are right, half if four. Questions 21 and 22 are Mezo scenarios: a safety failure on either (booking a red flag instead of directing to emergency care, advising on medicines, releasing results without consent) fails the test whatever the score, and the trainee re-reads the Safety Card.
- Accept any answer that carries the meaning in plain English. Spelling counts only where the question asks for it, or where the misspelling changes the word.
- Pass: 18 of 22 (about 80%). Below that, the trainee resits the same test the following Friday.
- Exercise: mark out of 5. 5 is client-ready; 3 is correct with gaps; 1 shows the section was not read.
- On the task: write the test score, the exercise mark, and one line on the term or idea to work on. Then approve, or request changes if the exercise needs redoing.
- Time: about ten minutes per trainee per week.

## The exam

### Written paper

- Questions: any ten from questions 1 to 19 of each test, 60 in all. Trainees have revised these, which is intended: the course is about command of the language, not surprise.
- Document to decode: Exam document A below. Trainees write it out in plain English and expand every abbreviation. Counts as 10 of the 60 marks; take 50 questions instead of 60.
- Pass: 48 of 60.

### Exam document A (written paper)

> 63M, k/c HTN & T2DM x 10 yrs. c/o polyuria, fatigue, BLE swelling x 3/52.
> O/E: BP 170/100, pedal oedema. Ix: RBS 14.2 mmol/L, HbA1c 9.1%, Cr 310 umol/L, Hb 9.8.
> Renal USS: bilaterally small kidneys. Dx: CKD (likely diabetic and hypertensive nephropathy), anaemia of CKD.
> Plan: refer nephrology, start dialysis assessment, optimise BP and glycaemic control. R/V 1/52.

- Key points: 63-year-old man, known hypertensive and type 2 diabetic for ten years; passing a lot of urine, tired, both legs swollen for three weeks; very high blood pressure, swelling of the feet; high sugar, poor three-month control, raised creatinine (poor kidney function), mild anaemia; both kidneys small on ultrasound; chronic kidney disease from diabetes and hypertension, with anaemia caused by it; referred to a kidney specialist, assessed for dialysis, blood pressure and sugar to be controlled better, reviewed in one week.

### Exam document B (oral, unseen)

> Dear Dr Okafor,
> Thank you for seeing this 29 yr old G1P0 at 34/40 with BP 160/110, proteinuria ++ and headache. Known HbSC. She was given MgSO4 and nifedipine stat and is NBM. Fetal heart present, CTG reactive. I would be grateful for your urgent review with a view to delivery, likely by LSCS under spinal. Blood group O Rh+, 2 units X-matched.
> Yours sincerely, Dr Bello, MO

- Key points: first pregnancy, 34 weeks; severe pre-eclampsia (high BP, protein in urine, headache); haemoglobin SC disease; given magnesium sulphate (to prevent fits) and a blood pressure drug immediately; nothing by mouth; baby's heart heard and monitoring normal; urgent review for delivery, probably caesarean under spinal; blood group known and two units ready. "MgSO4" is a do-not-use abbreviation on many safety lists, which a strong candidate may notice.

### Oral, 20 minutes each

- Clinical letter: give an unseen referral letter of about 150 words. Mark out of 5 for reading accuracy and plain-English explanation.
- Dictation: ten terms from the workbook. Floor: eight of ten. Below the floor the course is not passed and the oral is repeated in two weeks.
- Client explanation: two minutes on one client's clinical service. Mark out of 5.
- Glossary: check ten terms at random. Mark out of 5.
