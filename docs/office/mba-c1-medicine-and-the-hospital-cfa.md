## Week 1: How medical words are built

### 1.1 The method

Most medical terms are built from three parts: a prefix at the front, a root in the middle, and a suffix at the end. Learn about 150 parts and you can decode thousands of words.

| Term | Broken down | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Gastritis | gastr (stomach) + itis (inflammation) | Inflammation of the stomach |
| Hysterectomy | hyster (uterus) + ectomy (removal) | Removal of the uterus |
| Bradycardia | brady (slow) + cardi (heart) + ia (condition) | Slow heart rate |
| Arthroplasty | arthr (joint) + plasty (surgical repair) | Joint replacement or repair |
| Nephrology | nephr (kidney) + logy (study of) | The specialty of kidney medicine |

- Read from the end: the suffix usually tells you what kind of thing it is (a disease, an operation, a test). Then the root tells you where. Then the prefix tells you how much, which side, or when.
- Combining vowel: an "o" is often added to join parts: gastr + o + scopy = gastroscopy.

### 1.5 Words that trip people up

- Ectomy, otomy, ostomy: removing it, cutting into it, making a permanent opening. A laparotomy opens the abdomen; a colectomy removes the colon; a colostomy brings the colon out to the skin.
- Ileum and ilium: the ileum is part of the small bowel; the ilium is part of the pelvic bone.
- Prostate and prostrate: the prostate is a gland; prostrate means lying face down.
- Perineal and peritoneal: the perineum is between the legs; the peritoneum lines the abdomen.
- Aesthetic and anaesthetic: cosmetic work, and the drug that removes sensation.
- Mucus and mucous: mucus is the noun; mucous membrane is the adjective.

## Week 2: The body by system I

For each system: what it does, the vocabulary, the conditions you will hear about most in Nigerian hospitals, and the specialist.

### 2.1 Heart and circulation (cardiovascular)

- What it does: the heart pumps blood through arteries to the body and back through veins.
- Specialist: cardiologist (medical); cardiothoracic surgeon (surgical).

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypertension (HTN) | High blood pressure. The most common chronic condition in Nigerian adult clinics. |
| Blood pressure (BP) | Written as two numbers, for example 140/90 mmHg: systolic over diastolic |
| Myocardial infarction (MI) | Heart attack: heart muscle dies from blocked blood supply |
| Heart failure (CCF, CHF) | The heart cannot pump enough. CCF is congestive cardiac failure. |
| Arrhythmia | Abnormal heart rhythm |
| Stroke (CVA) | Cerebrovascular accident: brain damage from a blocked or burst blood vessel |
| Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) | A clot in a deep vein, usually the leg. A known risk after surgery. |
| Electrocardiogram (ECG) | Recording of the heart's electrical activity |
| Echocardiogram (echo) | Ultrasound of the heart |

### 2.2 Lungs and breathing (respiratory)

- Specialist: pulmonologist or chest physician.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asthma | Narrowing of the airways in episodes |
| Pneumonia | Infection of the lung tissue |
| Tuberculosis (TB, PTB) | Bacterial infection, PTB when it is in the lungs. A notifiable disease. |
| COPD | Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease |
| Upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) | Colds, sore throats, sinus infections |
| Dyspnoea, SOB | Difficulty breathing, shortness of breath |
| SpO2 | Oxygen saturation, measured with a clip on the finger. Normal is roughly 95% or higher. |

### 2.3 Gut and liver (digestive, gastrointestinal)

- Specialists: gastroenterologist (medical, does endoscopies); general surgeon (surgical).

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Peptic ulcer disease (PUD) | Ulcers in the stomach or duodenum |
| GORD | Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, acid reflux. GERD in American usage. |
| Hepatitis B and C | Viral liver infections. HBsAg is the hepatitis B screening test. |
| Cirrhosis | Scarring of the liver |
| Appendicitis | Inflamed appendix, usually an emergency operation |
| Haemorrhoids | Swollen veins at the anus, called piles |
| Upper GI endoscopy, gastroscopy | Camera into the stomach through the mouth |
| Colonoscopy | Camera into the large bowel |

### 2.4 Kidneys and urinary system (renal, urology)

- Specialists: nephrologist (medical, runs dialysis); urologist (surgical).

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chronic kidney disease (CKD) | Long-term loss of kidney function. Often follows hypertension or diabetes. |
| Acute kidney injury (AKI) | Sudden loss of kidney function |
| Dialysis | A machine does the kidneys' filtering. Haemodialysis is usually three sessions a week. |
| Urinary tract infection (UTI) | Infection of the bladder or kidneys |
| Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) | Enlarged prostate, common in older men |
| Renal transplant | Kidney transplant |

### 2.5 Blood (haematology)

- Specialist: haematologist.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anaemia | Low haemoglobin. Measured as Hb or PCV. |
| Sickle cell disease (SCD) | Inherited blood disorder. Genotype SS. Crises cause severe pain. |
| Genotype | AA, AS, SS, AC and others. Tested before marriage and in children. |
| Blood transfusion | Giving donated blood, measured in units or pints |
| Malaria | Parasite infection carried by mosquitoes. Tested by MP (malaria parasite) film or RDT (rapid test). |
| Leukaemia, lymphoma, myeloma | Cancers of blood cells, lymph tissue and bone marrow plasma cells |

### 2.6 Hormones (endocrine)

- Specialist: endocrinologist.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Diabetes mellitus (DM) | Raised blood sugar. Type 1 (no insulin made), type 2 (insulin not working well). |
| HbA1c | Average blood sugar over about three months |
| FBS, RBS | Fasting blood sugar, random blood sugar |
| Hypoglycaemia | Dangerously low blood sugar |
| Thyroid disease | Hyperthyroidism (overactive), hypothyroidism (underactive), goitre (enlarged gland) |

## Week 4: Who does what, and how they write it down

### 4.1 Doctors

| Grade | What it means |
|---|---|
| House officer (HO) | One-year internship after medical school, required before full registration with MDCN |
| Medical officer (MO) | A fully registered doctor not in specialist training. Most private hospital doctors are MOs. |
| Resident doctor: registrar, senior registrar | Doctors in specialist training in a teaching hospital |
| Consultant | A fully trained specialist. In private practice, the consultant usually brings the patients. |
| Fellow (FWACS, FMCS, FWACP, FMCP and others) | The postgraduate qualification of a consultant, from the West African College or the National Postgraduate Medical College |
| Medical director (MD) | The doctor who runs the hospital clinically. Often also its owner. |
| Chief medical director (CMD) | Title of the head of a federal teaching or medical centre |

- Address: Dr and surname unless told otherwise. A professor is Professor. A consultant surgeon may prefer Mr or Mrs in the UK tradition; follow what they sign.

### 4.2 Everyone else

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| Nurse, nursing officer | Ward and clinic care. Grades rise to chief nursing officer; the head is often the director of nursing or matron. |
| Midwife | Pregnancy and birth care. Many Nigerian nurses are dual qualified (RN, RM). |
| Pharmacist | Dispenses and checks medicines. Registered with the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (PCN). |
| Medical laboratory scientist | Runs lab tests. Registered with MLSCN. |
| Radiographer | Takes X-rays and scans. The radiologist (a doctor) reports them. |
| Physiotherapist | Rehabilitation |
| Anaesthetist | Doctor who gives anaesthesia. Nurse anaesthetists also work in many Nigerian hospitals. |

### 4.7 A discharge summary to decode

> Patient: Mrs A.B., 46 yrs. Adm: 12/10. D/C: 15/10.
> c/o: heavy menstrual bleeding x 8/12, dizziness. PMH: HTN on amlodipine 5mg OD.
> O/E: pale, BP 150/95, PR 102. Abdo: pelvic mass ~20/52 size.
> Ix: Hb 7.2 g/dL, PCV 22%. Pelvic USS: multiple uterine fibroids, largest 9 cm.
> Dx: symptomatic uterine fibroids with anaemia.
> Rx: transfused 2 units blood. Abdominal myomectomy 14/10 under GA. EBL 800 ml.
> D/C meds: ferrous sulphate 200mg TDS x 3/12, amlodipine 5mg OD, paracetamol 1g QDS PRN.
> F/U: gynae OPD 2/52 for wound review and histology.

- Adm and D/C are admission and discharge. PR here is pulse rate, not rectally: context decides.
- "x 8/12" means for eight months. "2/52" means in two weeks. "/7" is days, "/52" weeks, "/12" months.
- In pregnancy, "34/40" means 34 weeks of a 40-week pregnancy.
- "~20/52 size" means the uterus was the size of a 20-week pregnancy.
- EBL is estimated blood loss.

## Week 6: How a patient moves and how money moves

### 6.1 The outpatient journey

| Step | What happens | Where money moves |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Folder opened, details taken | Card or registration fee. HMO patients show their card. |
| Triage, vitals | Nurse checks BP, temperature, weight | |
| Consultation | Doctor sees the patient | Consultation fee, or HMO bills it |
| Investigations | Lab and imaging ordered | Paid before the sample is taken in most private hospitals. HMO patients may need an authorisation code. |
| Review | Doctor reads results, decides | Review fee |
| Pharmacy | Medicines dispensed | Paid at the pharmacy. HMO formulary limits what is covered. |
| Follow-up or referral | Next appointment, or sent elsewhere | |

### 6.2 The inpatient journey

| Step | What happens | Where money moves |
|---|---|---|
| Decision to admit | From OPD or A&E | Deposit demanded from cash patients. Authorisation code from the HMO. |
| Admission, clerking | Bed allocated, full history | Bed per day, feeding, nursing |
| Treatment, surgery | Procedures, theatre, drugs | Theatre fee, surgeon's fee, anaesthetist's fee, consumables, implants |
| Daily ward round | Consultant reviews | Daily consultant fee in many hospitals |
| Discharge | Summary, medicines, bill | Final bill settled before the patient leaves. Disputes and DAMA happen here. |
| Claim | Hospital bills the HMO | Payment comes weeks or months later. Rejections and underpayments are common. |

### 6.3 Payers in one line each

- Out of pocket: the patient or family pays cash. Still the majority of private hospital revenue in Nigeria.
- HMO: a health maintenance organisation manages insurance for employers. The hospital is paid a capitation (a fixed amount per enrollee per month for primary care) or fee-for-service against a tariff, after an authorisation code for anything significant.
- NHIA: the National Health Insurance Authority, which replaced the NHIS in 2022. Write NHIA, not NHIS.
- State schemes: for example the Lagos State scheme run by LASHMA.
- Corporate retainers: a company pays the hospital directly for its staff.
- Diaspora: family abroad paying for care at home.

C4, The Nigerian Health Market, goes into each of these. For now, know the words.

### 6.4 Quality and governance words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clinical governance | The system that keeps clinical care safe and improving |
| Morbidity and mortality (M&M) meeting | Doctors review complications and deaths to learn from them |
| Incident report | Written record of something that went wrong or nearly did |
| Clinical audit | Checking practice against a standard |
| Accreditation | External certification of quality, for example SafeCare in Nigeria |
| Standard operating procedure (SOP) | The written way a task is done |
| Infection prevention and control (IPC) | Hand hygiene, sterilisation, isolation |
| Informed consent | Agreement after the risks are explained |
| Medical negligence | Care below the accepted standard that causes harm |

### 6.5 Metrics vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bed occupancy | Share of beds filled, on average |
| ALOS | Average length of stay, in days |
| Throughput | Patients through a unit in a period |
| Theatre utilisation | Share of available theatre time used |
| Conversion | Share of consultations that turn into a procedure or admission |
| Payer mix | Share of revenue from cash, HMO, NHIA and others |
| Debtor days | How long the hospital waits to be paid, mostly by HMOs |
